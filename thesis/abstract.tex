\section{Abstract}
Chemical sensors are widely used in a variety of applications, including process control, agriculture, and more. Over the years, and with the advances in microelectronics fabrication process, various commercial gas sensors have emerged. Gas sensing was shown to be possible and possible to fabricate in commercial quantities. A unique approach for the implementation of a gas sensor using CMOS-SOI-MEMS is exhibited in the GMOS gas sensor, explained in detail including [] and [].
While GMOS has so far produced interesting prospects of further research and development in the realms of measurement selectivity and low energy costs. There is however a great need to improve the sensors accuracy. In this paper we focus on sensor sampling in AC in sub-threshold region. We propose a new and low-cost method to implement an analog readout circuitry based on a Lock-in-amplifier. First, the proposition is implemented in a lab environment using standard test equipment. Later, a read-out expansion board is implemented and tested. 
In summary:
\begin{itemize}
  \item This study focuses on improvement of the sensitivity performance of the GMOS gas sensor by investigating the sensor performance in an AC measurement, first by utilizing high end electronics lab equipment, and later in a low cost and portable readout system.
  \item The research further explores the noise factors which are applicable to the GMOS sensor, greatly improving the chance or the sensors.
\end{itemize}
