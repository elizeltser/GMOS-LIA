# P-08 Validation of the fast settings

## Goal

Confirm that the fast settings give the same settled VX and VY as the 1 s, 10 uF runs, so that P-09 to P-17 can use them.

## Method

**Settled window.** The fast run has 0.15 s of simulated time and 517 Hz. The settled window is the last 20 whole periods. It has a length of 38.6847 ms and runs from 111.3153 ms to 150.0000 ms. The reference run uses the last 50 whole periods, from 903.2882 ms to 1000.0000 ms (96.7118 ms). This follows the measurement rule of `docs/simulation-index.md`, with the window length set by the fast settings of the entry.

**Mean.** The mean is the time-weighted trapezoid mean over the raw PSpice points. The window ends are interpolated. This follows the workaround in `docs/simulation-quirks.md` for point means. A mean on a uniform 0.5 us grid agrees with the raw-point mean to 0.01 uV (10 nV) for VX and VY.

**Settling check.** The mean of the last window is compared with the mean of the window of the same length before it (72.6306 ms to 111.3153 ms for the fast run, corrected by the judge). The difference is reported in the table below.

**Settling bias.** For a first-order output that starts at 2.5 V, the window mean is V_ss + B, with B = (2.5 V - V_ss) F and F = (tau / Tw) (1 - exp(-Tw / tau)) exp(-t0 / tau). The output time constant is tau = 10 kOhm x 1 uF = 10 ms for the fast run, and tau = 10 kOhm x 10 uF = 0.1 s for the reference. With Tw = 38.68 ms and t0 = 111.3 ms, F = 3.706e-6. The bias is then 4.7 uV at 2 V, as the entry predicted. The bias is also estimated from the data as B = (m_last - m_prior) / (1 - exp(Tw / tau)). The corrected settled value is V_ss = m - B. The quoted reference values in the entry are corrected in this way, as the check below shows.

**Ripple.** The 1f and 2f components of the last window come from a least-squares fit of sinusoids on a 0.5 us grid. The mean does not use this fit. Over whole periods the 1f and 2f terms average to zero, so the fit gives the ripple only.

**Model for VY at p = 0.** The P-04 result for the Y channel is VY - 2.5 V = -K sin(d - delta) + c A^2, with K = 2A/pi, d = 0.1785 deg (from the entry, T/4 minus 482.6 us), delta = 126 ns = 0.02345 deg (the constant lag of P-04) and c A^2 with c A^2 = 0.457 mV at A = 2 V (the slew term of P-04, 111 ns at 2 V). The model is the same for both amplitudes, so the slew term scales with A^2.

## Discussion

**VX.** At 2 V, the corrected VX is 1.2267865 V. It is 0.0260 mV above the ideal value. A time-step error of 5 us gives a gain error of (n omega h)^2 / 12 = 2.2e-5 at the fundamental (see [P-05](P-05.md)). For 2 V this is a shift of +0.0280 mV. The fast run agrees with this model to 0.002 mV. The reference run gives +0.0400 mV (corrected) with the same step, which is 0.012 mV above the model. P-05 also found this excess for the reference run. The difference between the fast run and the reference (-0.0147 mV) comes from this excess. The fast run agrees with the time-step model, and the reference does not. The cause of the 0.012 mV excess is not identified. The 1 mOhm resistors and the window length are estimated below 1 uV, so the data do not point to them. The change of cout was not tested in a run.

At 0.2 V, the corrected VX is -127.3248 mV. This is 0.0009 mV from the ideal value. The 5 us gain error would give a shift of +2.8 uV at this amplitude. The measured shift is -0.9 uV, so the difference of 3.7 uV is below the resolution of this comparison. The check does not resolve the time-step error at 0.2 V.

**VY at 0.2 V.** The entry expected about -0.40 mV from the 0.18 deg Y timing error alone. The measured value is -0.3439 mV. The difference is +0.053 mV, or 13 %. The entry expectation was incomplete. It left out two terms that the P-04 analysis found:

- A constant lag of 126 ns (0.0234 deg). It shifts VY by +K sin(0.0234 deg) = +0.0521 mV at 0.2 V, which reduces the negative shift. It scales with the amplitude.
- A slew term of the Y demodulator, c A^2 = +0.0046 mV at 0.2 V. At 2 V it is 111 ns.

With these terms, the P-04 model gives -0.3400 mV at 0.2 V. The measured value differs from the model by -0.0040 mV (1.2 %). The constant lag accounts for +0.0521 mV of the shift, and the step effect discussed for 2 V below would account for about -0.0016 mV if it scales with A. The rest, about -0.002 mV, is not explained.

**VY at 2 V.** The same model gives -2.9883 mV at 2 V. The measured value is -3.0121 mV, so the residual is -0.0239 mV (0.8 %). This is just above the 0.02 mV level used for the reference check. The P-04 constants were fitted on the 20 us run of P-04. P-05 shows that VY changes with the maximum step. The 20 us baseline gives VY = 2.497005 V, and the 5 us reference gives 2.4969886 V. The step effect on VY is therefore -0.0160 mV from 20 us to 5 us. The residual of the model changes from -0.0067 mV at 20 us to -0.0239 mV at 5 us, a change of -0.0172 mV. The step effect explains about 93 % of this change. The likely cause is the step-size dependence of the constants that were fitted at 20 us. This is a supported explanation, not a verified one. A run of the P-04 model at 5 us would test it.

**Ripple.** The 2f ripple of VX at 0.2 V is 1.306 mV. This matches the P-01 value of 1.304 mV at 2 V and 10 uF, scaled to 0.2 V. The 1 uF output capacitor gives a 10 times larger ripple than 10 uF, so the 2 V value is 13.06 mV. The entry gave "about 13 mV" for the 0.2 V run. That is the 2 V value. The correct expectation for the 0.2 V run is about 1.3 mV. The VY ripple is 2.6 mV at 0.2 V and 26.1 mV at 2 V. This matches the P-01 value of 2.6 mV at 2 V and 10 uF, which is also a 10 times scaling. The 1f residuals (0.0059 mV for VX and 0.0195 mV for VY at 2 V) are larger than the P-01 values, which were below 0.001 mV at 10 uF. A 10 times larger filter gain at 1f accounts for a bound of 0.01 mV. The VX value is within this bound. The VY value is about twice it. The 1f terms do not change the window mean, because the window holds whole periods. Their cause is not identified.

**Settling.** The VX window differences follow the exponential model exactly. The VY window differences decay about 17 % slower than the VX differences. The last-window bias of VY is still at most 0.12 uV, so the settled values are not affected. The cause of the slower VY decay is not identified.

**Dispatcher numbers.** The preliminary numbers from the dispatcher differ from this analysis by at most 0.002 mV. The VX at 0.2 V agrees to 0.0001 mV. The VX at 2 V differs by 0.0009 mV, and the VY at 2 V by 0.0018 mV. The difference is most likely from the handling of the window ends. The dispatcher may have used the points inside the window without interpolating the ends. This is not verified.

## Open points

1. The entry's expected VY at 0.2 V (-0.40 mV) omits the constant lag (+0.0521 mV at 0.2 V) and the slew term (+0.0046 mV at 0.2 V). With these terms the expected value is -0.3400 mV. The judge should decide whether to change the entry. This analysis does not change the expected values.
2. The entry's expected 2f ripple of VX at 0.2 V is "about 13 mV". The 13 mV value applies at 2 V. The expected value at 0.2 V is 1.3 mV, and the measured value is 1.306 mV.
3. The reference values in the entry (VX 1.2268012 V and VY 2.4969886 V) are corrected 50-period means. The raw means are 1.2268980 V and 2.4969888 V. The entry should say that the values are corrected. The 0.7 uV difference between the quoted VX and the recomputed value comes from the rounding of the correction factor (7.6e-5 against 7.65e-5).
4. The quirk note in `docs/simulation-quirks.md` gives 2.4969897 V as the time-weighted mean of VY at 2 V. This run gives 2.4969879 V over the 20-period window (raw) and the same value on a 0.5 us grid. The quirk note does not state its window, so the two values cannot be compared exactly. The difference is 0.0018 mV. This is not verified.
5. The cause of the 0.012 mV excess of the reference VX over the time-step model (P-05 also reports this) is not identified. The fast run does not show it. A run of the fast circuit at cout = 10 uF for 1 s, with the same 5 us step, would test whether the excess comes from the circuit or from the reference run.
6. The P-04 model constants come from the 20 us run. The VY residual at 2 V is consistent with the step effect, but a model fitted at 5 us was not made.
7. The design-context check. `docs/design-context.md` section 4 gives the demodulator input as about 166 mV (Draft, from C-06). The entry uses 0.2 V, which is close to this value, as design issue D-09 recommends. No conflict. The demodulator frequency of 517 Hz is not in `docs/design-context.md`. Section 3 only gives a frequency range of 100 Hz to 10 kHz (Draft), and 517 Hz lies inside it. The 517 Hz value comes from the LIA netlist, so it is not a requirement.
8. The 1 mOhm resistors in `lia_v3_fast.cir` (`rser` and `rrail`) differ from the reference circuit. The effect is estimated below 1 uV. It was not checked by a run.
