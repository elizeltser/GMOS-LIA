# P-03 Offset sweep and dynamic range

## Goal

Find the input offset range over which the outputs stay correct, and describe the behaviour once the signal reaches the rails.

The raw points are 0.1 us apart near the reference edges and up to 20 us apart on the plateaus.

## Method

**Streaming parse.** `docs/sim-results/scripts/P-03_offset.py` reads each `.csd` file line by line, so the whole text is never held in memory. It writes one row per step to `sim-runs/<run>/<run>_steps.csv` and the last two periods of each waveform to `sim-runs/<run>/<run>_waves.npz`.

**Settled values.** The mean of each output over the last 50 whole periods of 517 Hz (96.71 ms) is taken on a 1 us grid by the trapezoidal rule. This is the method of P-01 and P-02. The settling check is the difference from the 50 periods before. The settling-bias correction of P-01 (`corrected = measured + 7.6e-5 x (measured - 2.5 V)`, at most 0.097 mV) is applied to the mean, the max and the min.

**Absolute and steady extremes.** The absolute max and min are taken over the 50-period window on the 1 us grid. These include the reference-edge transients, so they are not the steady range. The steady range is taken as the 0.1 and 99.9 percentiles of the same grid. These exclude the 0.1 % of samples with the largest excursions. The fraction of samples in transients is about 0.1 % to 0.2 % (see the transient section).

**Transients.** Two raw sections were streamed for the steps named in the review: P03a at ost = +0.40 V and P03b at ost = +0.25 V. The raw points inside 25 us of each extreme were printed. The reference edge was identified by the time modulo T/2. The X rising edge is at 50 ns and the X falling edge at T/2 + 150 ns (P-01). The Y edges are 482.6 us later. The script `docs/sim-results/scripts/P-03_extremes.py` gives the steady theory check and the 1 us grid comparison. Its raw-point check is in the transient section below.

**Max and min metric.** The max and min over the window are reported in the per-step tables, as the GUI metric of C-07 asks for.

**1f ripple.** The 1f amplitude is fitted on the two-period cache with sine and cosine terms at 517 Hz. The predicted value follows from the offset term of the demodulator output. This term is ost times the plus or minus 1 reference square wave, whose fundamental amplitude is (4/pi) ost. The RC filter (tau = 0.1 s) multiplies it by 1 / (2 pi f tau) = 3.080e-3.

**Departure point.** The deviation of each step from its ost = 0 value is taken. Starting at ost = 0, the walk goes outward on each side. The last ost inside the threshold, and the first ost outside it, are reported for thresholds of 1 mV and 0.1 mV.

Scripts: `docs/sim-results/scripts/P-03_offset.py` (parse, per-step tables, departure points, plots) and `docs/sim-results/scripts/P-03_extremes.py` (steady extremes against theory, transient search on the raw record, 1f check). Run them from the project root.

The X transient is an edge overshoot, and its size is set by the step 2 |ost| at each X edge. The Y transient is a dip on the same kind of edge at p = 90 deg. The edge geometry follows from the input phase. At p = 0 deg the X edges sit at the input zero crossings and the Y edges at the input peaks. At p = 90 deg the roles swap, so the X edges sit at the input peaks and the Y edges at the zero crossings. The 50-period absolute extremes are the largest of these edge values over all edges in the window. For this reason they depend on the time points of the record and not only on the circuit.

Two values of the grid-based listing are not confirmed. The 0.90 V and -1.05 V excursions quoted for other edges in the listing are not checked against raw points, and they are treated as grid artefacts. Not verified.

### Ripple and derived 1f term

The 2f ripple of V(VX) stays at 1.304 mV for |ost| <= 0.5 V in run P03a. It drops to 0.7 mV at |ost| = 2 V, where the demodulator clips. The 1f component is 0.001 mV at ost = 0. It grows linearly with |ost| near zero. This term explains why the peak-to-peak ripple grows from 2.7 mV at ost = 0 to 5.9 mV at |ost| = 0.5 V.

| Run and channel | Measured 1f per |ost| (mV/V), |ost| from 0.1 to 0.4 V | Predicted (mV/V) | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| P03a, V(VX) | 3.9193 to 3.9213 (mean 3.920) | 3.9196 | 0.0 % to 0.05 % |
| P03a, V(VY) | 3.9131 to 3.9268 (mean 3.920) | 3.9196 | -0.2 % to +0.2 % |
| P03b, V(VX) at \|ost\| = 0.25 V | 3.9149 and 3.9253 | 3.9196 | -0.1 % and +0.1 % |
| P03b, V(VY) at \|ost\| = 0.25 V | 3.9211 and 3.9178 | 3.9196 | 0.0 % and -0.1 % |

The prediction is (4/pi) / (2 pi f tau) = 1.2732 x 3.0781e-3 = 3.9196 mV per V of offset. The measurement agrees within 0.2 %. The residual spread is the sampling of the two-period fit, not a missing term. This closes the 1f open point of the earlier draft.

## Discussion

### Steady range and the clipping level

The steady demodulator range follows the theory of the X and Y outputs up to |ost| = 0.4 V. The lower end stops at a floor of about 0.01 V (negative side) and 0.02 V (positive side) from |ost| = 0.5 V on. The steady upper end of the Y output at p = 90 deg reaches 4.98 V to 4.99 V from |ost| = 0.5 V on, where the theory value 4.5 V + |ost| equals the supply of 5 V. The X output at p = 0 does not reach its upper end, since its steady maximum is 2.5 V + |ost| and stays below 4.5 V in the sweep.

The steady lower floor of run P03b does not exist at |ost| = 2 V. The steady minimum at +2 V is 0.5099 V (p 0.1), which matches the theory value 0.5 V. The earlier claim of a floor at |ost| = 2 V for run P03b came from the absolute minimum, which is an edge dip. That claim is withdrawn.

The steady ceiling in run P03a is not reached, because its steady maximum never exceeds 4.5 V in the sweep. The absolute maximum of 4.98 V at |ost| = 2 V in run P03a is an edge overshoot, not a ceiling. That claim is withdrawn.

### Which element limits first

The circuit between the input source and the demodulators is as follows. V4 is an ideal source, so it cannot clip. Its output is the node N00890, with no series element. N00890 feeds three things. It feeds S1 of the switch X_U1 (the input branch). It feeds R_R2 (10 kOhm) to the inverting input N00610 of U2. It feeds R_R4 (10 kOhm) to the Y side. The switch X_U1 passes N00890 (SEL low) or the 2.5 V bias of V_V3 (SEL high) to D (N00695), which is the non-inverting input of U2. The switch rails are VDD = VDC (5 V) and VSS = 0 V. U2 runs from VDC and ground. Its output is VX_DEMOD. The feedback is R_R1 (10 kOhm). The VY channel repeats this structure with X_U3, U4 and VY_DEMOD.

The elements that could limit first are the following. (a) The switch signal range. The model does not state a signal range for S1, and its supply pins are 0 V and 5 V. (b) The common-mode input range of U2 at IN+ (N00695) and IN- (N00610). The model does not state it (`vendor/README.md`). (c) The output swing of U2. The model does not state it either. The steady output floors measured here are about 9 mV to 21 mV from ground and about 10 mV to 20 mV from VDC.

The data point to (c) as the limit that is seen. The steady floor value does not change with the input excursions beyond the rails. For run P03a the input reaches -1.5 V at ost = -2 V and 6.5 V at ost = +2 V. The steady output stays at the floor and the theory ceiling in both cases. The floor changes by only 2.5 mV between ost = -0.5 V and -2 V. A switch or input common-mode limit on the input path would show a change in the steady output with the same input excursion, and no such change appears.

The data cannot exclude (a) and (b). Both would start at |ost| = 0.5 V, where the input touches 0 V or 5 V. The steady floor on the positive side starts at ost = +0.5 V, and there the input maximum is 5.0 V. The negative floor starts at ost = -0.5 V, where the input minimum is 0 V. The onset of the floor therefore coincides with an input rail on both sides. To separate them, V(N00890) and V(N00695) must be probed in a short run at ost = -0.6 V and +0.6 V.

### The absolute extremes come from the edges

The absolute extremes of the 50-period window are set by the reference-edge transients. At ost = +0.40 V the largest raw excursion is an overshoot of +0.37 V at the falling X edge (3.27 V against 2.90 V). At ost = +0.25 V the largest raw excursion for VY_DEMOD is a dip of -0.26 V at the falling Y edge (1.99 V against 2.25 V). The overshoot grows with the step 2 |ost| at the edge, but not linearly. Its amplitude depends on the record time points. Absolute extremes are therefore not a valid measure of the steady range.

### Max against mean

The max over the last 50 periods does not give the same departure point as the mean. At 1 mV the VX max departs at 0.3 V, and the mean at 0.5 V. At 0.1 mV the VX max departs already at 0.1 V. The reason is the 1f term. It grows as 3.92 mV per volt of |ost| (derived above). It adds to the peak of the 2f ripple, so the max moves by the amount of the 1f term, not by the mean. The max metric is therefore a finding of the ripple, and not a test of the circuit.

The GUI metric of C-07 is `max(V(VX))`. The archived `.dat` file was not opened, as the brief asks. A qualitative comparison is therefore not made. The C-07 run started at 0 V, so its whole-run max is not directly comparable with the last-50-period max used here. Not verified.

### Open asymmetries

Two features are not explained.

The VY departure at p = 0 is asymmetric. On the positive side the deviation is +0.58 mV at +0.5 V and +1.05 mV at +0.6 V. On the negative side it is +0.30 mV at -0.5 V, -0.09 mV at -0.7 V and +2.5 mV at -0.8 V. V(VY_DEMOD) was not probed in run P03a, so the Y demodulator output cannot be compared with theory. The Y reference edges at p = 0 deg sit near the input peaks, where the edge overshoot of the Y output is of the size of the step 2 |ost|. This may cause the asymmetry, but it is not verified.

The VX departure at p = 90 deg is -15.4 mV at +1.0 V and +15.5 mV at -1.0 V. The sign flips with ost, so the term is odd in ost. VX_DEMOD was not probed in run P03b, so the X demodulator output cannot be compared with theory. At p = 90 deg the X reference edges sit at the input peaks, where the input would exceed 5 V at ost = +1.0 V (peak 5.5 V) and fall below 0 V at ost = -1.0 V (trough -0.5 V). This is a candidate cause, but it is not verified. A clip at the input rail would not give an odd term in ost by itself. The duty-cycle asymmetry of V2 and V6 (100 ns, P-01) gives a shift of about 0.1 mV, which is too small. Not verified.

## Open points

1. The source of the 1f term is closed (derived, 3.92 mV per V, measured 3.92 mV per V within 0.2 %).
2. The VX settling check of +0.287 mV at ost = -1.25 V in run P03b is an outlier. The neighbouring steps show less than 0.07 mV. The raw record near that step should be checked for a sampling effect.
3. The VX deviation of -15.4 mV at +1.0 V in run P03b, and the odd sign in ost, are not explained. VX_DEMOD was not probed in that run. A run at p = 90 deg with V(VX_DEMOD) and V(N00890) probed at ost = +1.0 V and -1.0 V would show the steady X output and the input peaks.
4. The VY asymmetry at p = 0 deg (departure at -0.7 V and +0.5 V in the mean) is not explained. V(VY_DEMOD) was not probed in run P03a. A run at p = 0 deg with V(VY_DEMOD) and V(N00890) probed, at ost = -0.8 V, -0.5 V, +0.5 V and +0.8 V, would give the missing waveform.
5. Input clipping is not separated from output clipping. V(N00890), V(N00695) and V(N00610) must be probed in a short run at ost = -0.6 V and +0.6 V. Until then the output swing of U2 is the limit that the data show, and the switch and input common-mode limits remain possible.
6. The absolute extremes depend on the record time points near the edges. A run with a 0.1 us maximum step during the edges (as in P-01 run D) would give the edge values without interpolation. Not run.
7. The qualitative comparison with the GUI plot of C-07 is not made. The archived `.dat` file was not read.
8. The offsets of VX (+0.46 mV) and VY (+0.97 mV) from the ideal values in the flat range are the P-01 open points. They are not new here.
