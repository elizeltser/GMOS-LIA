# P-04 Dynamic range versus input amplitude

## Goal

Check that VX - 2.5 V is proportional to the input amplitude, and find the largest amplitude before clipping.

## Method

**Settled values.** The settled value of each channel is the mean over the last 50 whole periods of 517 Hz (96.71 ms). The raw points are interpolated onto a uniform 1 us grid, and the trapezoidal mean is taken on that grid.

**Settling check.** The same mean is taken over the 50 periods before the last window. The raw difference between the two windows is the settling check.

**Settling-bias correction.** The `.IC` start at 2.5 V leaves a decaying tail. The P-01 correction is 0.097 mV at |V - 2.5 V| = 1.27 V, so it is proportional to |V - 2.5 V|. It is applied at every step:

corrected = measured + 7.6e-5 x (measured - 2.5 V)

The correction is 0.0005 mV at 10 mV amplitude, 0.097 mV at 2 V and 0.133 mV at 3 V.

**Ideal theory.** VX - 2.5 V = -(2/pi) A and VY - 2.5 V = (2/pi) A sin(-d), with d = 0.1785 deg as the entry states.

**Fits.** The fit model is a + b A + c A^2, with a the offset, b the gain and c the square-law term. The fit uses the seven steps with A up to 2 V. The fit of a linear model alone is also reported. The linear region is then checked against the steps above 2 V.

**Demodulator extremes.** The 0.1 and 99.9 percentiles of V(VX_DEMOD) are taken on the 1 us grid over the last 50 periods. The absolute minimum and maximum over the same window are listed as well.

## Discussion

### The 0.46 mV deviation at 2 V is a gain error

The VX fit gives an offset of -0.003 mV and a square-law term of -0.0008 mV/V^2. Both are zero within the fit precision. The gain is -0.6363846 V/V, which is 0.0369 % below -2/pi. The error therefore scales with the amplitude, as a gain error does. A constant offset would not do this. This answers the open point of P-01 and P-02. The 0.46 mV VX deviation at 2 V is a gain error of about -0.037 %.

The gain deficit is close to the 0.039 % found in P-02 and the 0.036 % found in P-01. The settling correction changes the value by about 0.008 %. Without the correction, the raw deficit is about 0.044 %. The three values agree within the correction uncertainty.

The P-02 slew model predicts no square-law term in VX at p = 0. The fit agrees, since c_X is zero within the fit precision.

### The square-law term appears in the Y channel only

The VY fit gives c = +0.114 mV/V^2. The P-02 slew model predicts a Y term at p = 0 with c = 0.1025 mV/V^2 (from 0.41 mV at 2 V). The P-02 fit gave 0.480 mV at 2 V, which is c = 0.120 mV/V^2. The measured value lies between the two. The square-law term is therefore a slew effect of the Y demodulator, as P-02 proposed.

At 2 V, the VY error of +0.97 mV from the ideal value has two parts. The square-law term gives +0.46 mV. The linear gain gives +0.52 mV.

The linear part is a constant lag reduction. Write VY - 2.5 V = K sin(-phi), with K = 2/pi per volt of amplitude and phi the effective lag. The fitted gain b = -0.0017237 V/V gives sin(phi) = 0.0017237 / 0.636620, so phi = 0.1551 deg. The ideal lag is d = 0.1785 deg. The effective lag is therefore 0.0234 deg smaller, which is 126 ns at 517 Hz. The P-02 extended fit gives a constant Y phase of +0.156 deg, which matches the fitted 0.155 deg.

The square-law part adds a lag reduction too. At 2 V it is 0.457 mV / 1.27324 V = 0.0206 deg, or 111 ns. The two parts add to 0.0234 deg + 0.0206 deg = 0.044 deg, or 236 ns at 2 V. The P-01 value is 235 ns. The 235 ns of P-01 is therefore the constant delay (126 ns) plus the slew part at 2 V (111 ns).

### The linear region and the clipping

The steady lower end of the demodulator follows the input trough, 2.5 V - Vampl, up to 2.25 V. The input trough reaches 0 V at Vampl = 2.5 V. Above that amplitude, the steady demodulator output stops at a floor of about 0.007 V to 0.008 V. The P-03 sweep found the same floor, near 0.009 V.

The VX mean departs from the linear fit at the same amplitude. At 2.75 V, the mean is 51 mV above the linear value. At 3 V, it is 156 mV above. The sign agrees with the floor. A demodulator output that cannot fall below 0.007 V has a mean that lies above its unclipped value.

The data do not separate the limiting element. The input trough and the demodulator floor both start at 2.5 V. This is the same problem as in P-03. The switch input range or the common-mode range of the op amp could also be the cause. The steady floor changes by only 1.3 mV between 2.5 V and 3 V, while the input excursion below 0 V grows from 0 V to -0.5 V. This points to the output stage, as P-03 found. The data do not exclude the input path.

The VY output departs more gently. Its deviation stays near 0.8 mV at 2.5 V and 2.75 V, and 1.2 mV at 3 V. Its ripple grows from 6.2 mV p-p at 2 V to 8.6 mV p-p at 3 V.

### The small-signal floor

The fitted offset is smaller than 0.01 mV for both channels. The offset is below 0.01 mV, and its source is not separated. The entry lists charge injection (QINJ = 0.4 pC in the switch model) and the op amp offset as candidates. This run does not tell them apart.

## Open points

1. Closed. The linear Y part (0.52 mV at 2 V) is a constant lag reduction of 0.0234 deg (126 ns). The square-law part (0.46 mV at 2 V) is 111 ns. Their sum, 236 ns, matches the 235 ns of P-01. See the Discussion.
2. The VY deviation of about 0.8 mV at 2.5 V and 2.75 V, and 1.2 mV at 3 V, is not explained. The VY output is non-monotone above 2.25 V.
3. The limiting element above 2.5 V (switch input, op amp input or op amp output) is not separated, as in P-03. VX_DEMOD and V(N00890) would need to be probed in a short run at Vampl = 2.75 V to check it.
4. Open. The 99.9 percentile drop of the upper steady edge is 1.7 to 1.8 mV per V. A V-cusp model with the measured flank slope (A x 2 pi x 517 Hz, confirmed at 3.24 mV/us per V) and two cusps per period predicts 1.55 mV per V. The difference is 14 %. A 0.1 us grid gives the same percentile, so the 1 us sampling is not the cause. The top 5 mV of the waveform is not a clean V (slopes of 0.5 to 2.7 mV/us near the peak, and overshoots to 2.51 V), so the cusp model does not hold at the top. The cause is not identified.
5. The small-signal offset is below 0.01 mV, but its source is not separated. Charge injection and op amp offset are not told apart by this run.
6. The wall time and the job time of each step are not verified. Only the last step is in the `.out` file.
