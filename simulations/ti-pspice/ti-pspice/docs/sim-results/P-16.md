# P-16 Overload recovery

## Goal

Measure how long the chain takes to return to within 1 mV after an input burst that drives the INA828 beyond the rails.

The netlist is `circuits/chain_ss.cir` with `asin` = 15 mV at f_osc, `asq` = 0 and `cout` = 1 uF. The output RC in the run is 10 kOhm x 1 uF = 10 ms. The design RC is 0.1 s. The `.out` file of P16_2u ends with the job statistics and has no `ERROR` line (checked with `grep`). It has the known warnings for the pulse edge defaults (ORPSIM-15459) and the ITL4 speed level (ORPSIM-16534).

## Method

**Reference and difference.** The section with `aburst` = 0 is the reference. The section with `aburst` = 0.5 V is the overload. Both are interpolated onto a uniform 2 us grid from 95 ms to 300 ms. The difference d(t) = V(overload) - V(reference) is formed for each probe.

**Settling check.** Before the burst the two runs must be identical. The largest difference for t < 96.7118 ms is 0.0000 uV for all five probes. The difference in the window from t_end + 12 ms to t_end + 31.3 ms has a mean of 0.0000 mV for HPF and VDIFF, so no offset is left at that time.

**Recovery time (entry rule).** The recovery time is the last time after t_end at which |d| is at least 1 mV, measured from t_end. The raw points are used, so the 2f ripple of the outputs counts. This follows the rule of the entry. A second measure uses a centred one-period mean of d. Near t_end, the centred window contains burst samples for about one period. Its values in that interval are therefore not usable. The envelope column is given only as a check.

**Fast transients.** The high-pass and low-pass ring at about 518 Hz with decay constants near 0.6 ms. The one-period mean does not follow such ringing, so a centred sliding maximum of |d| over one period gives the envelope. This envelope is used for the decay fits. The fit window is t_end + 1 ms to t_end + 4 ms for HPF and VSAMPLE. This starts one period after t_end, so no burst sample enters the fit.

**VX and VY prediction.** Once VSAMPLE has recovered at t_VS, the outputs decay with tau = 10 ms (the run, cout = 1 uF). With dV the difference at t_VS, the prediction is t_pred = t_VS + tau ln(dV / 1 mV). dV is the raw difference at t_VS. The centred-mean value differs by 0.2 % and gives the same result to 0.01 ms. The fitted decay constant is the log-linear fit of the envelope from t_VS to the measured recovery time.

**Scaling to tau = 0.1 s.** The RC part of the recovery time is scaled by 10 (from 10 ms to 0.1 s) with dV kept fixed. This is an arithmetic scaling, not a run. The limit of this scaling is discussed below.

**Linear check.** The high-pass and low-pass stages are linear networks with ideal op amps. Their free-response poles follow from the netlist element values. The poles are found from the nodal equations of `chain_ss.cir` (script section 5). The low-pass transfer function is H_LP(s) = g21 g22 / (g21 g22 + s C3 (g21 + g22) + s^2 C3 C4). The simulated HPF difference is passed through H_LP with `scipy.signal.lsim`. The result is compared with the simulated VSAMPLE difference. This replay tests whether the low-pass op amp adds memory of its own.

## Discussion

**Recovery times.** VSAMPLE and HPF recover within 6.2 ms, which is below the 10 ms expected by the entry. VDIFF recovers within the burst end. The slow recovery of VX and VY (71.8 ms and 51.9 ms) is the output RC of the demodulator. The sensor chain has recovered by then. The overload leaves VX shifted by about 1.1 V and VY by about 0.17 V at t_end. The shifts decay with tau = 10 ms, as the fits show (9.999 ms and 9.997 ms).

**Saturation memory.** The high-pass stage clips at the rail during the burst. Its output differs by 2.3 V at t_end (`V(HPF)`), which is consistent with clipping at a rail (not checked against the node voltages). The memory of this clipping decays within about 5 ms. Its decay constant of 0.63 ms agrees with the linear pole of 0.62 ms, so the charge on C1 and C2 relaxes through the normal high-pass time constants. No slow component remains. VDIFF recovers within 0.1 ms, so the INA828 shows no visible memory at the 1 mV level. The replay shows that the low-pass stage adds no memory of its own. The replay reproduces VSAMPLE within 0.3 mV at 2 ms and the recovery within 0.002 ms. The sensor chain therefore has no saturation memory beyond its linear time constants.

**Decay constants.** The linear poles give 0.617 ms and 0.603 ms. The index estimate of Q/(pi f0) with Q = 1 gives 0.616 ms. This matches the first pole to 0.2 % and the second to 2 %. The fit to the HPF envelope gives 0.627 ms, which agrees. The VSAMPLE envelope fits give 0.72 ms to 0.96 ms. A linear cascade cannot decay more slowly than its slowest pole, so these fits do not give a pole time constant. The replay gives the same fit value (0.720 ms) from the linear low-pass applied to the simulated HPF. So the simulated VSAMPLE follows the linear network. The difference comes from the fit method. The two modes at 517.8 Hz and 523.7 Hz beat at about 6 Hz, and a sliding maximum over one period does not track such a beat exactly. This explanation is not verified in a separate test.

**Scaling to tau = 0.1 s.** The scaled value of 662 ms is an arithmetic scaling with dV held fixed. This is not right. The VX shift during the burst depends on tau. A first-order argument gives a smaller shift at tau = 0.1 s. The shift is proportional to 1 - exp(-T_burst / tau), where T_burst = 19.3 ms. This factor is 0.86 at tau = 10 ms and 0.18 at tau = 0.1 s. A smaller dV gives an earlier recovery. So the scaled value is probably an overestimate. A rough estimate gives about 0.5 s to 0.6 s. This estimate is not verified. A run with `cout` = 10 uF (the design RC) is needed.

**Clipping.** The overload is 0.5 V differential, so the INA828 output swings by 5.5 V before clipping. The high-pass output clips at the rails. The overload level is a test value. The design sensor range is TBD (`docs/design-context.md`, section 4).

**Method notes.** The one-period centred mean of a step response is not usable within one period after the step, because the window contains the step. The sliding maximum or the raw points should be used there. This is not in `docs/simulation-quirks.md`. It may be added by the dispatcher or the judge.

## Open points

1. The design RC (tau = 0.1 s, `docs/design-context.md`, section 5) was not simulated. The scaled recovery of 662 ms is an estimate. A run with `cout` = 10 uF, and the same burst, would give the real value. The judge may want a new index entry for this run.
2. The acceptable recovery time after an overload is TBD (`docs/design-context.md`, section 5, "Output bandwidth or settling time", requirement TBD). The 1 mV threshold is the entry's choice. The user must set both.
3. The overload level is a test value. The design sensor range is TBD (`docs/design-context.md`, section 4). The 0.5 V differential is not a design value.
4. The 5 us runs failed to converge (see `docs/simulation-quirks.md`). The 2 us run is used. Recovery times at a finer step were not checked (not verified).
5. The VSAMPLE envelope decay (0.72 ms to 0.96 ms) disagrees with the linear poles (0.603 ms). The cause is probably the fit method. This is not verified.
6. The entry's 4.7 ms per stage (from 2.5 V to 1 mV) assumed a 2.5 V overload at each stage. The simulated VSAMPLE starts at 315 mV and recovers in 6.2 ms. The linear replay gives the same time. The entry's per-stage estimate does not apply to this start level.
