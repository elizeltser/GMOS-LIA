# P-09 Full chain on a single 5 V rail

## Goal

Run the sensor stand-in through the INA828, both filters and the demodulator on one 0 V to 5 V rail. Check the INA828 input and output against its supply limits and check that its inputs sit at 2.5 V.

## Method

**Settled window.** The run has 0.15 s of simulated time. At 517 Hz the window is the last 20 whole periods, 38.6847 ms long, from 111.3153 ms to 150.0000 ms. The prior window of the same length is from 72.6306 ms to 111.3153 ms. The index rule uses 50 periods for the 1 s runs. The fast settings of the batch use 20 periods (`docs/simulation-index.md`, "Fast settings").

**Mean.** The mean is the time-weighted trapezoid mean of the raw PSpice points, with the window ends interpolated. This follows the point-mean quirk in `docs/simulation-quirks.md`. A uniform 0.5 us grid is printed as a check. For VX and VY the grid and the trapezoid means differ by at most 0.02 uV in the window.

**Settling check.** The last window is compared with the prior window. For a first-order output with tau = 10 ms (R3 = 10 kOhm, cout = 1 uF), the window mean is V_ss + (V_0 - V_ss) F, with F = (tau / T_w)(1 - exp(-T_w / tau)) exp(-t_0 / tau). The model gives F = 3.706e-6. The bias is also estimated from the data as B = (m_last - m_prior) / (1 - exp(T_w / tau)). The settled value is m_last - B.

**Stage transfer.** A least-squares fit of a DC term and the 1f to 3f sinusoids on the 0.5 us grid of the same window gives the complex amplitude of each node. Ratios of nodes give the stage gain and phase. The fit uses the differential input V(INP) - V(INN) as the reference.

**Ideal values.** The ideal chain is H(f) = 11 V/V x H_HP(f) x H_LP(f). H_HP and H_LP are the Sallen-Key transfer functions of the stage values in `circuits/chain_ss.cir`. They reproduce the entry values |H(517 Hz)| = 11.0793 V/V and +1.630 deg. The demodulator values follow from the ideal demodulator formulas of the common definitions in `docs/simulation-index.md`, with the sine amplitude A = 15 mV (`asin`):

- VX - 2.5 V = -(2A/pi) |H| cos(p + phi)
- VY - 2.5 V = -(2A/pi) |H| cos(p + phi + omega tau), with tau = 482.6 us, which is the Y reference delay in `V_SELY`

**Duty and switch-delay terms.** The quirk `docs/simulation-quirks.md` ("PULSE width is counted after the rise time") gives -0.103 mV/V for the reference duty and -0.048 mV/V for the switch delay. Both terms multiply the DC offset of V(VSAMPLE) relative to 2.5 V.

**Common-mode sweep.** In P09b, the output VDIFF is compared with the target 2.5 V + 11 x 10 mV = 2.61 V. The band is 1 mV wide.

## Discussion

**The dispatcher's 2.402 V.** The dispatcher quoted a VX window mean of 2.402 V at p = 0 deg. This analyst could not reproduce that number for a settled window. The window means are 2.394300 V (last 20 periods) and 2.394321 V (prior 20 periods). The number 2.402 V is the mean over the whole run, from 0 s to 150 ms. The full-run mean is 2.402215 V (time-weighted) and 2.402244 V (point mean). The start of the run at 2.5 V adds about 7.9 mV to that mean. So the 2.402 V value is a whole-run average, not a settling error. The run is 15 tau long. A window that starts after 11 tau is settled to about 0.4 uV, which is well below the 5 uV settling bias of the batch. The run length is adequate, and no re-dispatch is needed for settling.

**Cause of the VX and VY differences.** The residuals are small and they are consistent with each other. The stage fits localise them:

- The INA828 gain is 11.00007 V/V. Its phase at 517 Hz is -0.06 deg. Its gain is correct.
- The high-pass stage has a gain of -0.031 % and a phase of -0.04 deg below the ideal Sallen-Key values.
- The low-pass stage has a gain of -0.025 % and a phase of -0.03 deg below the ideal values.

The total front-end phase is therefore about 0.13 deg less than the ideal value. A phase deficit of 0.126 deg (-0.0022 rad) moves VY at p = 0 deg, and VX at p = 90 deg, by about R x Delta phi = 105.7 mV x (-0.0022 rad) = -0.23 mV. The observed residuals are -0.19 mV in both cases. The difference of 0.04 mV is about 0.02 deg. The VX and VY estimates of the phase (1.527 deg and 1.528 deg) are 0.024 deg above the VSAMPLE fit (1.504 deg). That offset matches the 126 ns constant lag of P-04 (0.023 deg), which the VSAMPLE fit does not include. This match is not verified by a separate run.

The VX residual at p = 0 deg, +0.057 mV, is the amplitude deficit of -0.055 %. It is not a settling effect.

**Time step.** The P-17 sine run uses the same chain with `.TRAN 0 0.15 0 2u`. It can be compared with P09a, which uses 5 us. The sine results differ as follows:

| Quantity | P09a (5 us), p = 0 deg | P17sin (2 us), p = 0 deg | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| VX - 2.5 V (mV) | -105.7002 | -105.7053 | -0.005 |
| VY - 2.5 V (mV) | 2.4881 | 2.5113 | +0.023 |
| Total gain at 517 Hz (V/V) | 11.07321 | 11.07316 | -0.0005 % |
| Total phase at 517 Hz (deg) | 1.504 | 1.516 | +0.012 |

The 5 us step moves VY by 0.023 mV. The phase moves by 0.012 deg. The gain moves by less than 0.001 %. Most of the deficit (-0.055 % gain and -0.13 deg phase) stays with the 2 us step. So the time step is not the main cause of the deficit. The fast settings use 5 us for the 0.15 s runs, which costs about 0.02 mV on VY. This is consistent with the 0.003 % step error in `docs/simulation-quirks.md`, but a 5 us run is not accurate to better than about 0.02 mV on VY.

**Remaining cause.** The residual -0.05 % gain and -0.13 deg phase come from the model of the amplifiers or the switches. The candidates are the finite gain and bandwidth of the OPAx192 followers and the INA828 bandwidth. The INA828 phase of -0.06 deg is measured here. The op amp bandwidth is not checked. The model values were not read, so the source is not verified.

**Common-mode range (D-02).** The INA828 model (`C:\CDS_SPB_HOME\CDSSETUP\PSPTILIBDIR\INA828.LIB`, lines 387 to 392) has common-mode clamp sources at 2 V from the supply pins (VCM_MAX = VCC - 2 V and VCM_MIN = VEE + 2 V). The sweep edges at vcm = 2.00 V and 3.00 V agree with these clamps. So the model gives the same common-mode range as the D-02 statement (about 2 V from each rail). The INA828 datasheet section 7.3.3 was not checked (not verified). The 2.5 V common mode of the chain is 0.5 V from each edge of the model range. The 1 mV band lies about 0.01 V to 0.02 V inside the clamp edges.

When vcm falls outside the 2.0 V to 3.0 V range, the output falls back to the 2.5 V reference. The output does not clip at a rail. A failure of the common-mode range therefore removes the signal and does not show as a rail clip. The lock-in would then read the output as zero.

**Sensor model.** The sensor model is TBD in `docs/design-context.md` section 1. The 15 mV signal level is the Draft value of section 4, which the entry cites.

## Open points

1. The dispatcher's 2.402 V was the mean of the whole run, from 0 s, and it is not a settled value. The dispatch note should record the window used.
2. The source of the -0.055 % gain and -0.13 deg phase deficit is not identified. The OPAx192 and INA828 bandwidth and open-loop gain were not read from the models (not verified). A test with an ideal op amp in the chain would separate the two.
3. The P-09 entry does not give limits for VX and VY separately. The entry should set them, for example as the R limit and the phase limit, so that a fail is not a judgement made after the run.
4. The INA828 datasheet section 7.3.3 was not checked (not verified). The model clamps agree with D-02, but the datasheet should be read before a design decision.
5. The 5 us step leaves 0.02 mV of uncertainty on VY. The fast settings with 5 us are adequate for R and for VX. A 2 us run is needed if VY must be known to better than 0.02 mV.
6. The P09b edges are given on a 0.02 V grid. A finer sweep near 2.00 V and 3.00 V would give the edges to 1 mV.
