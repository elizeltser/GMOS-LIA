# P-05 Harmonic response using V7

## Goal

Measure how the demodulator responds to harmonics of the input frequency.

## Method

**Settled values.** The settled value of each channel is the mean over the last 50 whole periods of 517 Hz (96.71 ms). Every harmonic n times 517 Hz also has a whole number of periods in this window, so the ripple of each harmonic averages out. The raw points are interpolated onto a uniform 1 us grid, and the trapezoidal mean is taken on that grid.

**Settling check and bias correction.** The settling check is the raw difference between the last 50 periods and the 50 periods before. The `.IC` tail is removed with the P-01 correction, as in P-04: corrected = measured + 7.6e-5 x (measured - 2.5 V).

**Baseline.** In the 20 us analysis (Results and Comparison with expected, below) the baseline is the P-04 run at Vampl = 2 V, with no harmonic: VX = 1.227224 V and VY = 2.497005 V. In the time-step analysis (from Time-step convergence on) the baseline of each harmonic run is its own n = 2 step, as the judge asked. The n = 2 step is an even harmonic and has an expected effect of zero. The fundamental of each step comes from the P-04 run at the same maximum step.

**Derivation of the expected deltas.** The input minus 2.5 V is x(t) = 2 sin(wt) + 0.2 sin(n wt). For a reference q(t) of +1 and -1, the demodulator gives VX - 2.5 V = -<x q_X> and VY - 2.5 V = -<x q_Y>. This sign convention reproduces the P-01 result VX - 2.5 V = -(2/pi) A cos p. A harmonic term is a_h sin(n wt). Its mean with a centred square reference of duty D, centre t_c, follows from the Fourier series of the reference, c_n = (4/(n pi)) sin(n pi D).

The reference centres are set by the measured delays:

| Channel | Centre of the high pulse | Effective phase | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| X | T/4 + 100 ns, phase delay of 0.023 deg | phx = -0.023 deg | P-02 extended fit |
| Y | 0.859 us early with respect to T/2 (from the 482.6 us delay and the 100 ns edge offset), plus the switch delay of 36 ns | theta_Y = 0.1564 deg | Derived from the baseline, see below |

The Y phase is derived from the baseline at 2 V. The Y output is VY - 2.5 V = -K sin(theta_Y) + S_Y, with K = 2A/pi = 1.273240 V and the slew term S_Y = +0.480 mV (P-02, p = 0). This gives sin(theta_Y) = (0.480 mV + 2.995 mV) / 1.273240 V = 2.729e-3, so theta_Y = 0.1564 deg. This matches the P-02 fit of 0.156 deg. At 2 us the baseline VY gives 0.1570 deg. The change is 0.4 % of the Y phase, and it changes the predicted harmonic deltas by less than 0.001 mV.

The expected deltas for odd n are:

| Channel | Formula | Values (mV) for n = 3, 5, 7 |
|---|---|---|
| VX | dVX_n = -(2 AH / (n pi)) cos(n omega s_X) | -42.44, -25.46, -18.19 |
| VY, timing | dVY_n(timing) = -(2 AH / (n pi)) (-1)^((n-1)/2) sin(n theta_Y) | +0.3475, -0.3475, +0.3475 |
| VY, slew | dVY_n(slew) = 2 (S_Y / A) AH sin(n pi / 2) | -0.0960, +0.0960, -0.0960 |
| VY, total | timing plus slew | +0.2515, -0.2515, +0.2515 |

The X delay factor cos(n x 0.023 deg) equals 1.0000 for all n. The Y timing term has the same magnitude for every odd n, because the 1/n weight cancels the factor sin(n theta_Y) / n, which is close to theta_Y. The Y reference samples the input at the edges, where the harmonic has the same full size for every odd n. The slew term follows the edge amplitude. The Y edges sit at the input peaks, where the edge amplitude is A + AH sin(n pi / 2). The slew shift scales with the square of this amplitude, which gives the factor 2 in the slew formula.

For even n, the reference has a small content at even harmonics, c_n = 4 eps (-1)^(n/2) with eps = 100 ns x 517 Hz = 5.17e-5. This gives:

| Channel | Formula | Size at n = 2 and 4 |
|---|---|---|
| VX | dVX_n = -2 AH eps sin(n omega s_X) | below 1e-5 mV |
| VY | dVY_n = 2 AH eps (-1)^(n/2) sin(n theta_Y) | below 0.0003 mV |

The expected even-n deltas are therefore zero within 0.001 mV.

**Time-step model.** A reference edge sampled with a linear interpolation on a maximum step h gives a relative gain error of about (n omega h)^2 / 12 for the harmonic n, with omega = 2 pi f_osc. For n = 1 at h = 20 us this gives 0.035 %, close to the P-01 deficit. The model is taken from the judge note of the index entry.

**Analyst limits.** The entry gives no numeric pass limits. The analyst uses the limits below. The judge may change them.

| Check | Analyst limit |
|---|---|
| Odd-n delta against theory | 1 % of the theory value (VX) |
| Odd-n VY residual | 0.05 mV |
| Even-n delta | 0.02 mV |
| Rejection of VX against 1/n | 0.2 dB |

## Time-step convergence

The maximum step was reduced from 20 us to 5 us and 2 us. The fundamental and the odd harmonics were run at each step, with all other settings unchanged.

### Fundamental gain deficit

The deficit is 1 - (VX - 2.5 V) / (-K), in %. A positive value is a loss of gain.

| Maximum step | VX at 2 V (V) | VX minus ideal (mV) | Deficit meas (%) | Model (n = 1) (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 us | 1.2272249 | +0.4645 | 0.0365 | 0.0352 |
| 5 us | 1.2268012 | +0.0408 | 0.0032 | 0.0022 |
| 2 us | 1.2267667 | +0.0062 | 0.0005 | 0.0004 |

The ideal VX is 2.5 V - K = 1.2267605 V. The model is (n omega h)^2 / 12. The fundamental deficit falls by 11.4 times from 20 us to 5 us, and by 75 times from 20 us to 2 us. The model gives 16 and 100. At 5 us the measured deficit is about 0.001 % (0.012 mV) above the model. The judge quick check gave the same VX values to within 0.5 nV.

## Discussion

### The VX gain loss is a time-step error

The first analysis found a VX loss that grew with n at 20 us. The time-step runs show the cause. The loss falls with the maximum step, from 0.31 % (n = 3) at 20 us to 0.0065 % at 2 us. The fundamental loss falls from 0.037 % to 0.0005 %. The VX loss of the harmonics is therefore the linear interpolation error of the 20 us step, not a property of the demodulator. The measured convergence is slower than the h^2 model, with the measured ratios of 47 to 54 against 100 between 20 us and 2 us. The residual is a small absolute error of 0.001 mV to 0.004 mV. Its source is open.

The C-03 check did not show this step dependence, because it used four-digit output.

### The gain deficit of P-01, P-02 and P-04

The 0.037 % to 0.039 % gain deficit of P-01, P-02 and P-04 is this time-step error. At 20 us the VX deviation at 2 V is +0.46 mV (0.036 %). At 2 us the same deviation is +0.006 mV (0.0005 %). P-01 open point 1, the unexplained +0.46 mV of VX, is closed by this result. The VX value at 2 us equals the ideal value to within 0.006 mV.

The P-02 magnitude deficit of 0.039 % is seen in both channels at 20 us. The Y part of this deficit at 2 us is not tested, because the phase sweep was not repeated at 2 us. The index statement that P-02 is graded A still uses the 20 us run, and its gain number should be read as the 20 us value.

### The VY response is set by the Y timing, not by 1/n

The VY deltas change by about 0.02 mV between 20 us and 2 us, and they match the timing plus slew theory to 0.01 mV at 2 us. The Y reference samples the input at its peaks, so the harmonic error is the same size for every odd n, as Derivation of the expected deltas shows. The 1/n rule does not apply to VY.

### Even harmonics

At 2 us the VX response to n = 2 is zero to 0.0001 mV against the fundamental. The +0.018 mV of the 20 us run was a step artefact. The VY response to n = 2 is +0.007 mV, which is above the expected zero but within the 0.02 mV limit.

### Y phase and the slew term

The Y slew model is a first-order estimate, linear in the edge amplitude. At 2 us it gives residuals of +0.009 mV (n = 3) and +0.008 mV (n = 7). The sign and size of the slew term are therefore supported at this level. It is not verified by a probe of the Y edges.

## Open points

1. Closed. The VX loss of the harmonics at 20 us is the maximum-step error. It falls to 0.0065 % (n = 3) and 0.036 % (n = 7) at 2 us. Section Time-step convergence gives the data.
2. Closed. The +0.018 mV VX offset at n = 2 (20 us) disappears at 5 us and 2 us.
3. The measured convergence of the odd-harmonic deficit is slower than (n omega h)^2 / 12. The ratios are 47 to 54 instead of 100 between 20 us and 2 us. The residual is 0.001 mV to 0.004 mV in absolute terms. The source is not identified.
4. The VY residual of the even harmonic n = 2 is +0.007 mV to +0.010 mV at 5 us and 2 us. It is not explained.
5. The Y slew term for the harmonic is a first-order estimate. A probe of V(N01577) and VY_DEMOD at a Y edge with n = 3 would test it. This is not run.
6. The VY settling differences at n = 4 (-0.009 mV) and n = 7 (-0.014 mV) at 20 us are above 0.01 mV. The time-step runs have no n = 4 step, and the 2 us n = 7 settling difference is not separately reported here.
7. The entry expects 1/n for both channels. The data show 1/n for VX only. The entry is not changed here. The judge should decide on the wording.
8. The per-step job time is not verified. The job times in Runs are the times from the file timestamps given by the coordinator, not from the `.out` files.
