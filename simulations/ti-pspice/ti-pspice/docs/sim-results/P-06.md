# P-06 Non-harmonic interference using V7

## Goal

Check that an interferer at a frequency that is not a harmonic gives no DC error, only a filtered ripple.

## Method

**Settled means.** The settled value is the mean over the last 50 whole periods of 517 Hz (96.7118 ms, from 0.903288 s to 1.000000 s). The mean uses a trapezoidal rule on the 1 us grid, as in P-04. The P-01 settling-bias correction is applied:

corrected = measured + 7.64e-5 x (measured - 2.5 V)

The settling check compares this window with the 50 periods before it.

**Beat residual.** The 50-period window is not an integer number of periods for the products. For a product at frequency f, its mean over the window is Re[z (exp(j 2 pi f) - exp(j 2 pi f T1)) / (j 2 pi f W)], with W = 96.7118 ms. The script evaluates this for each fitted product with its fitted complex amplitude z.

**Least-squares fit.** The fit uses the window 0.5 s to 1.0 s, and the window 0.8 s to 1.0 s as a check. The model is:

y(t) = c0 + c_e exp(-t / 0.1 s) + sum over f_k of [c_k cos(2 pi f_k t) + s_k sin(2 pi f_k t)]

The exponential term is the decay of the IC transient. Its time constant is the RC of the output filter, 0.1 s. The fitted c_e equals the IC minus the settled value (2.5 V - 1.2272 V = 1.2730 V for VX), so the model describes the transient correctly. The frequencies are 2 f_osc (1034 Hz), 4 f_osc (2068 Hz) and the products |f_i +/- n f_osc| for n = 1 and 3. The 4 f_osc term is added after a first pass, see Discussion.

**Expected ripple.** The output is minus the mean of the input times the square-wave reference, filtered by the RC filter. The reference harmonic n has amplitude 4/(n pi), so a product of an interferer A_i at f_i has amplitude 2 A_i / (n pi) at |f_i +/- n f_osc|. The filter gain is |H(f)| = 1 / sqrt(1 + (2 pi f tau)^2) with tau = 0.1 s.

The Y reference is delayed by 482.6 us. Its harmonic n therefore has an extra phase n theta, with theta = 2 pi x 517 Hz x 482.6 us = 89.82 deg. For a difference product (f_i - n f_osc), the Y minus X phase is +n theta. For a sum product (f_i + n f_osc), it is -n theta. A negative frequency reverses the sign. The script computes the complex amplitudes with these rules.

For the fundamental, the 2 f term comes from n = 1 and n = 3 together, and the 4 f term from n = 3 and n = 5 together. Their X amplitudes add with the sign of the weights. Their Y amplitudes add as complex phasors, so the Y amplitude differs from the X amplitude (see Discussion).

## Discussion

**No DC error.** A sine at f_i times a square wave has zero mean when f_i is not a harmonic of the reference. The measured settled values show no DC error above 0.03 mV at either step. The data agree with the expected result.

The residual DC shifts are 0.007 mV to 0.022 mV. They do not have the same sign at the two steps (VX is -0.007 mV at 50 Hz and +0.015 mV at 600 Hz), so they do not look like a fixed bias. The source is not identified. A second-order (square-law) term of the demodulator could move a small part of the interferer into DC. The P-04 analysis found such a term in VY, but this link is a hypothesis and it was not tested.

**Window choice.** The 50-period window is good enough. The only product with a notable window residual is 83 Hz, and it has 8.027 cycles in the window. This gives a residual of at most 0.008 mV. A longer window is not needed for the 0.1 mV limit.

**Y reference and the fundamental harmonics.** The statement of the entry, that the Y reference shifts the product phases but not their amplitudes, holds for every product that comes from one reference harmonic. These are all the interference products in this run. It does not hold for the 2 f and 4 f terms of the fundamental. Each of these comes from two reference harmonics, and the Y reference changes their relative phase. The X terms subtract (2 A/pi - 2 A/(3 pi) = 4 A / (3 pi)). The Y terms add (phase shifts of 90 deg and 270 deg, giving 8 A / (3 pi)). The measured Y 2 f term is 2.611 mV, about twice the X value of 1.304 mV, and the fit agrees with the phasor sum.

**The 4 f term.** The first fit used only 2 f and the products. Its residual was 0.10 mV rms for VX and 0.42 mV rms for VY, the same in the baseline. The spectrum showed a line at 2068 Hz (4 f_osc). It comes from the fundamental with n = 3 and n = 5 and is not an interference product. Its expected amplitudes are 0.1307 mV for VX and 0.5226 mV for VY, and the fit gives 0.1299 mV and 0.5221 mV. Adding this term to the fit reduces the residual to 0.029 mV rms for VX and 0.20 mV rms for VY.

**Gain at 1 kHz.** The VX ratios at 1034 Hz and 2068 Hz are 0.998 and 0.994. The VY ratios are 0.999 at both frequencies. The product at 1117 Hz is 0.9986 for VX. The gain of the products at 467 Hz and 83 Hz is 1.000 and 0.999. The deficit at 1 kHz is small, but it is larger than the fit precision. The cause is not identified. A finite op amp bandwidth or a small error of the filter model are possible causes. Neither was tested.

**Neglected products.** The fit uses n = 1 and n = 3. The n = 5 products of the interferer lie at |f_i +/- 5 f_osc|. Their expected amplitudes are 0.016 mV and 0.015 mV for 50 Hz (at 2535 Hz and 2635 Hz), and 0.020 mV and 0.013 mV for 600 Hz (at 1985 Hz and 3185 Hz). These are all below 0.02 mV and do not change any conclusion.
