# P-44 DAC staircase through the gate filter

## Goal

Show how close the gate signal is to a pure sine for the F303 DAC staircase, and which part of the remaining distortion the filter can and cannot remove. Measure the gate amplitude, THD, THD+N to 20 kHz, the ZOH images and the residual from a fitted sine, for several sample counts, the 8-bit mode, the maximum amplitude, a realistic DAC output (settling, buffer off) and DAC code errors.

## Method

**Window and grid.** The window is 10 T to 30 T at 518 Hz, from 19.305 ms to 57.915 ms (1 T = 1.9305 ms). The node voltages are interpolated linearly from the saved CSDF points onto a uniform 0.1 us grid of 386100 points. These points span 38.61 ms. Twenty periods are 38.61004 ms, so the window is 38.6 ns short of an integer number of periods.

The script checks the 38.6 ns shortfall in three ways. It compares the least-squares fit on the short window with a least-squares fit on an exact 20-period grid. It also compares both with FFTs over exactly 20 periods, at 25600 and 51200 points per period. The GATE lines of every run agree with the short-window fit to at most 0.00003 uV on every fitted line. For the EXC_DAC harmonics h3, h5 and h7 the shortfall moves the fit by 0.004 to 0.009 uV at 1.2 V, and by up to 0.037 uV at N = 32 (h3 is 17.3302 uV on the short window and 17.3470 uV on the exact grid). The cause is attributed to out-of-basis ZOH image lines that leak into the fitted harmonics at about 1e-6 of their amplitude. This estimate is not isolated line by line. The EXC_DAC image lines at 2N ± 1 f0 differ more, and their difference comes from the grid dependence of the FFT and not from the window (see the FFT reference section). The gate is not affected at the reported digits.

**Fit.** A least-squares fit gives the DC level, and sine and cosine amplitudes at k f0 for k = 1 to 20. It also gives the images at N f0 ± f0 and 2 N f0 ± f0. Amplitudes are peak values. Phases refer to t = 0, with the convention that an ideal sin(omega t) has phase 0. The same fit is applied to EXC_DAC, GATE and, for P44b, EXC_IDEAL.

**Metrics.**
- THD is the root sum square of h2 to h20 divided by h1.
- THD+N to 20 kHz is the rms of the residual in the band 0 to 20 kHz, divided by the fundamental rms. The residual is the gate minus the fitted DC and fundamental. The band power comes from an FFT of the window, with a brick wall at 20 kHz. The residual is used instead of the gate itself so that the 2.5 mV fundamental does not leak into the bins next to f0.
- The residual rms is the time-domain rms of the gate minus the fitted DC and fundamental, without a filter. The FFT band power of the residual equals its time-domain rms (0.5994 uV for P44a_N128, both values).
- The image levels are the fitted peak amplitudes at N f0 ± f0 and 2 N f0 ± f0, in dBc relative to the fundamental of the same node.

**Settling.** The fit is repeated on the first 10 periods and on the second 10 periods of the window. The check compares the fundamental amplitude and phase. The check of a mean against the window before it cannot be made, because the 60 ms run has no 20-period window before 19.305 ms.

**Max step and edges.** The transient uses a maximum step of 1 us. The staircase edges are PWL breakpoints. For every staircase run, 100 % of the edges (k ts and k ts + 100 ns) have a saved solver point within 5 ns. The median saved step in the window is 1000 ns for N = 32 and N = 64, 966 ns for N = 128 and 128 ns for N = 256.

**Level rounding.** `gen_exc_stair.py` writes each PWL level with `{v:.6f}`, which is 1 uV resolution. The script checks that the levels in `circuits/afe_eval_P44_N128.cir` equal round(code times LSB, 6) over one period, with a maximum difference of 0.000000 uV. It then computes the closed form with the levels rounded to 1 uV as a side check.

**Closed form.** The functions of `P-44_expected.py` are executed from the file, without its prints and Monte Carlo. The closed form uses exact codes, the zero-order hold and the ideal MFB with R59 and 50 pF. It adds the 100 ns edge factor, sinc(m f0 tau) exp(-j pi m f0 tau). This changes the phase of line m by minus m times 0.0093 deg and the magnitude by less than 1e-6. The script checks that the netlist values of R5, R6, R7, C6, C7, R59 and cgate equal the constants of the closed form. It also checks that a series 15 kOhm at EXC_DAC equals R6 = 135 kOhm.

**Measured transfer.** For buffer on, H is V(GATE) divided by V(EXC_DAC). The AC source is 1 V, so this is the gate over the source. For buffer off, H is V(GATE) over the source (1 V). The measured H is interpolated linearly in log f, with magnitude in dB and unwrapped phase. The grid is 10 Hz to 1 MHz with 100 points per decade.

**GOUT check and output stage fit.** The script takes the ratio V(GATE)/V(GOUT) and compares it with the R59 and 50 pF pole. It compares V(GOUT) with the ideal op-amp MFB without R59. It then solves the MFB nodal equations with a single-pole op amp (gain-bandwidth GBW) and an output impedance Ro, with the gate load R59 plus the gate capacitor. A grid search over GBW (0.2 to 2.5 MHz) and Ro (0 to 1000 Ohm) minimises the rms dB error against the measured GOUT from 20 kHz to 1 MHz. The GBW and Ro values are fitted values, not taken from the op-amp model.

**Floor.** The floor is the THD of the ideal SIN run P44_ref, measured with the same window, grid and fit.

**Monte Carlo.** The code-error Monte Carlo uses the same seed (44), the same loop order and the same error model as `P-44_expected.py`. The model is a combined one. Each board has independent code errors uniform in ±1 LSB, plus a bow and an S-curve. Each shape has an amplitude uniform from 0 to 4 LSB and a random sign. The rows named "bow 4 LSB" and "S-curve 4 LSB" use a fixed amplitude of 4 LSB with a random sign, so one board is enough for them. The script reproduces the ideal values of `P-44_expected.py` to four decimals. The measured H replaces the ideal H in the second set of results.

The finite GBW alone, with a single pole at 1.1 MHz and Ro = 0, gives a response slightly below the ideal one. At 66.07 kHz it gives -86.67 dB, against an ideal value of about -86.60 dB. Output impedance is needed to reproduce the measured excess.

The GBW 1.1 MHz pair with Ro = 500 Ohm leaves differences of -0.26 dB at 50 kHz, -0.35 dB at 66 kHz, -0.45 dB at 100 kHz and -0.19 dB at 200 kHz. The best fit over the full grid has an rms error of 0.25 dB over 20 kHz to 1 MHz. Its GBW of 0.50 MHz and Ro of 275 Ohm are fitted values, which the data does not pin down closely.

The evidence points to feedthrough from the input network (R6, the C7 node and R5) into an output impedance that rises with frequency. The fit does not prove the mechanism, and the op-amp model (OPAx196) is not compared directly. This is not verified.

## Discussion

**What the filter removes.** The filter removes the ZOH images that sit near multiples of N f0. At N = 128 the image at 127 f0 (65.8 kHz) is -42.08 dBc at EXC_DAC and -83.09 dBc at GATE, so the filter removes about 41 dB. At N = 256 the image at 255 f0 (132 kHz) is removed by about 47 dB. At N = 32 the image at 31 f0 (16.1 kHz) is removed by only about 20 dB, because the MFB is already falling at that frequency. Above 20 kHz the removal is limited by the output stage excess described in the GOUT section.

**What the filter cannot remove.** The MFB is flat within 0.2 dB up to 2.6 kHz, which is the fifth harmonic. The response is -0.19 dB at 2.59 kHz in the ideal model and -0.17 dB in the measurement. Every harmonic below the corner passes at the same level as at the source. The distortion at h2 to h20 therefore comes from the source. At N = 128 the gate THD of 0.031 % is set by h3, h5 and h7 (0.36, 0.54 and 0.18 uV at the gate). In the simulation they come from 12-bit quantization alone (0.320 LSB rms periodic error). On a real board, DAC INL and DNL add harmonics at the same low orders. The filter cannot remove either. The 8-bit mode has a quantization step 16 times larger, and its THD is 0.61 %.

**DAC code errors.** The code-error model in `P-44_expected.py` is an assumption, not the F303 datasheet shape. The combined model is defined in the Method. With it, the 95th percentile THD at N = 128 is 0.120 % at 0.3 V and 0.088 % at 1.2 V. Both values are below the 0.3 % requirement, with a margin of about 2.5 times at 0.3 V.

**Image at the gate.** The excess is at GOUT, not in the gate network. V(GATE)/V(GOUT) matches the R59 and 50 pF pole within 0.00000 dB. GOUT is above the ideal-op-amp MFB by 0.48 dB at 20 kHz, 4.06 dB at 66 kHz, 7.36 dB at 100 kHz and 34.4 dB at 1 MHz. The same 4 dB excess appears in P-26 at 66 kHz.

A finite GBW alone does not explain it. A single pole at 1.1 MHz with Ro = 0 gives -86.67 dB at 66 kHz, which is slightly below the ideal value. An output impedance that rises with frequency is needed. With GBW 1.1 MHz and Ro = 500 Ohm, the model gives -79.48, -82.89, -86.89 and -90.76 dB at 50, 66, 100 and 200 kHz. The measurement gives -79.21, -82.54, -86.44 and -90.57 dB. The GBW and Ro values are fitted values, not taken from the op-amp model. The mechanism is feedthrough from the input network (R6, the C7 node and R5) into that output impedance. This is not verified against the op-amp model.

The image at N = 128 is -83.09 dBc in the simulation. The closed form with the measured MFB gives -82.99 dBc, which is within 0.11 dB. The index table uses the ideal MFB and gives -87.02 dBc. The table value is therefore an error in the expected value, not a failure of the staircase or the filter.

**Realistic DAC.** The RC of 0.5 us and 1 us delays the fundamental by 0.092 deg and 0.185 deg, as the RC-only estimate predicts. The loading of the MFB input adds about 0.001 deg and reduces the gain by 0.83 %. The buffer-off case reduces the gain by 11 % (to 4.446 mVpp), exactly as the closed form with R6 = 135 kOhm predicts. Its phase and corner follow the same closed form.

**Floor at 1.2 V.** The floor lines and the source lines add as vectors on each harmonic. Their phases are not known, so the THD lies between two bounds. The bounds use the P44_ref floor for h2 to h7 scaled by 4, because the floor is linear in amplitude. They use the closed-form lines at 1.2 V with the levels rounded to 1 uV, and a floor of zero for h8 to h20. The anti-phase bound is 0.00707 % and the in-phase bound is 0.00972 %. The measured 0.00896 % lies inside this range, so the 1.2 V result is consistent with the floor. For comparison, a root sum square of the closed-form THD (0.00731 %) and the floor (0.00455 %) gives 0.00861 %.

**Settling.** The fundamental differs by less than 0.00002 % between the two halves of the window in every run. The 60 ms run is therefore settled at the level of the entry.

## Open points

1. The gate image at N = 64, 128, 256, for 8 bit and for 1.2 V fails the 1 dB limit against the ideal-MFB entry values. These entry values are an error in the expected values, because they assume an ideal op amp. The closed form with the simulated MFB agrees within 0.22 dB. The owner should update the expected values to the measured-MFB closed form, or state that the requirement uses the ideal MFB. The same question was raised for P-26.
2. The P44c magnitude is 0.5 dB above the ideal formula at 20.9 kHz and 4 dB above it at 66 kHz. This is a vendor-model deviation from the ideal formula, not a board failure. The GOUT evidence points to feedthrough into a rising output impedance. The GBW and Ro values are fitted, and the op-amp model is not compared directly.
3. The 8-bit mode gives 0.61 % THD. It does not meet the 0.3 % requirement. The owner should confirm that the 12-bit mode is required for the gate.
4. The owner should confirm the floor clause of the entry. The 1.2 V THD of 0.00896 % lies outside the ±20 % band of the entry value 0.007 %, and it lies inside the vector bound of 0.00707 % to 0.00972 %.
5. The general measurement rule of `simulation-index.md` (last 50 whole periods) cannot apply to a 60 ms run. The entry window of 20 whole periods is used, as the entry states. This is consistent with the batch rule of `design-context.md`, which uses 20 periods for the 518 Hz runs.
6. The EXC_DAC closed-form difference is closed. It comes from the 1 uV rounding of the PWL levels. The rounded closed form matches the PSpice lines at h3, h5 and h7 to 0.002 uV. The window shortfall moves the EXC_DAC harmonics h3 to h7 by up to 0.037 uV at N = 32, and it does not change the gate lines (0.00003 uV). The FFT estimate of the EXC_DAC image lines at 2N ± 1 f0 depends on the FFT grid and is not converged at 51200 points per period, so the exact-grid least-squares fit is the reference for these lines (see the FFT reference section).
7. The design values in the entry match `simulations/design-context.md` for the excitation frequency (518 Hz, current), the 5 mVpp default gate amplitude and the MFB gain of -1/120 (current). The 50 pF sensor load matches the sensor load of the same file. The 0.3 V pk excitation, the THD limit of 0.3 %, the image limit of -80 dBc, the 1.2 V firmware maximum and the references AN-4 and AN-7b are not found in `design-context.md` by search. These values are not verified against it.
8. The index quotes the firmware sample count N = 128 from `firmware/firmware_requirements.md`, which describes the STM32U575, not the F303. This is not verified for the F303.

## Not verified

- The F303 DAC datasheet values (DocID026415): settling 3 us typical and 4 us maximum, buffer output range 0.2 V to VDDA - 0.2 V, DNL ±2 LSB, INL ±4 LSB, and output impedance 15 kOhm with the buffer off. These were not read from the PDF in this session.
- The code-error model (independent ±1 LSB errors plus a 4 LSB bow or S-curve). It is an assumption, not a measured DAC.
- The op-amp model of the MFB (OPAx196). Its GBW and output impedance were not compared with the vendor data. The GBW and Ro values in this summary are fitted.
- The requirement values quoted in the entry (0.3 % THD, -80 dBc image, 20 mVpp firmware maximum, AN-4, AN-7b), which are not in `design-context.md`.
