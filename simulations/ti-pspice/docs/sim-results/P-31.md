# P-31 Power-up with the ±15 V input first (SIM-18 for the board)

## Goal

Show that no op amp latches or stays at a rail when the board is switched on with J4 (±15 V) first and J5 (±5 V) second (DC-3, E-12). Check that the TMUX6136 ESD path carries no current in that order. The references run from t = 0, as from a NUCLEO that is already on.

## Runs

| Run | Command | Simulated | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| P31_powerup | `python tools/run_sim.py circuits/afe_eval_P31.cir --name P31_powerup --probe "V(P15V)" "V(M15V)" "V(P5VA)" "V(M5VA)" "V(BPF_OUT)" "V(X_OUT)" "V(Y_OUT)" "V(GATE)" "V(VSUP_A)" "V(D1)" "V(D2)" "V(REF_X)" "V(REF_Y)" "V(KEBP)" "V(KED1)" "V(KED2)" "V(KEX)" "V(KEY)" "V(KEBN)" "V(KEN1)" "V(KEN2)" "V(KENX)" "V(KENY)" --extra ".OPTIONS NUMDGT=8" --timeout 7200` | 3 steps (tramp = 1, 5, 20 ms), status done, solver time 1625.1 s, no ERROR line | `sim-runs/P31_powerup/P31_powerup.csd` |

The circuit is `circuits/afe_eval_P31.cir`. It is `circuits/afe_eval.cir` with u15 = u5 = 0, t15 = 10 ms, t5 = 50 ms, cpf = 15 nF and `.TRAN 0 0.3 0 5u`. It has ten ESD stand-ins, one from each TMUX6136 (U24) signal pin to each rail. BPF_OUT is the analog source pin S1A and S2A (pins 2 and 10), D1 and D2 are the drain pins (pins 3 and 11), and REF_X and REF_Y are the digital select inputs SEL1 and SEL2 (pins 1 and 9). Each stand-in is a DSCHOTTKY diode in series with a 1 Ohm resistor. The current of a stand-in is the voltage across its resistor.

The datasheet treats the two pin types differently (TI SCDS397B, revised August 2025, §5.1). The analog pins Sx and D have an absolute maximum of VSS - 0.3 V to VDD + 0.3 V and ±30 mA. The select pins SEL1 and SEL2 have GND - 0.3 V to VDD + 0.3 V and ±30 mA. The positive stand-ins (each pin to P15V) therefore represent all five pins. The negative stand-ins ENX and ENY (M15V to REF_X and REF_Y) do not represent the device, because the lower limit of the select pins refers to GND and not to VSS. They never conduct in this run, because the references do not go below 0 V.

Earlier runs that failed or were diagnostic are not used for the results. The first attempt, P31_powerup, probed each diode current and failed with ORPSIM-16583. The short runs P31_test_v and P31_bisect_* found the cause, which is recorded in `docs/simulation-quirks.md`.

The analysis scripts are `docs/sim-results/scripts/P-31_powerup.py` (X_OUT and Y_OUT, rails, ESD peak over the run) and `docs/sim-results/scripts/P-31_esd.py` (ESD split into start-up, edge and DC parts, forward-bias check, charge balance, time constants). Run both from `simulations/ti-pspice`.

## Method

**Time step.** The maximum step is 5 us (`.TRAN 0 0.3 0 5u`). The reference edges are 10 ns (t_slope), and the solver places points on them.

**Resampling and means.** For the window means and the settling check the solver points are resampled to a uniform 1 us grid. Window means use the cumulative trapezoid integral, so each window is exact. Peaks, the start-up charge and the diode voltages are taken from the raw solver points, because the 1 us grid misses the 10 ns edges. (Judge correction, 8 November 2026: the first version took them from the 1 us grid and gave a start-up peak of 2.26 A instead of 2.45 A, edge peaks of 8.9 mA instead of up to 18.7 mA and a charge of 30.8 uC instead of 32.0 uC.)

**Final value.** The final X_OUT and Y_OUT are the mean over the last 20 whole periods (38.61 ms at 518 Hz) of the run, which ends at 0.3 s.

**Settling.** The one-period boxcar mean of X_OUT and Y_OUT is compared with the final value. The 2f0 ripple averages out in the boxcar. The settling time is the last time at which the boxcar mean differs from the final value by more than 1 mV.

**ESD stand-in current.** The current is (V at the K node minus V at the other node) divided by 1 Ohm. The peak is taken over the whole run. It is also split into three parts: the peak before P15V reaches 5 V (start-up), the peak after that (rails up, including the switching edges), and the mean over the last 20 periods (DC, leakage). The charge is the trapezoid integral of the raw current.

**Forward bias.** The diode voltage of each stand-in is V(anode) minus V(K node). A stand-in conducts forward only when this voltage is positive. This separates conduction from the two model artifacts of DSCHOTTKY: the displacement current of its 100 pF CJO at the edges, and its reverse current -IS = -5 uA.

**Rails.** The distance of each op-amp node to the rails is taken over the window after the J5 ramp.

**Time constants.** The boxcar mean of each node after the J5 ramp is fitted with an exponential in the band from 2 % of the initial deviation down to 2 mV.

### Start-up current through the stand-ins

Before P15V reaches 5 V the reference pins feed the unpowered +15 V rail. The table gives, for each positive stand-in, the largest current, the largest diode voltage and the charge in the interval before 10 ms (J4 still off). The values are the same for the three tramp values.

| Stand-in | Peak current before 10 ms | Largest diode voltage | Duration above 1 mA (ms) | Charge before 10 ms (uC) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EX (REF_X, SEL1, to P15V) | 2.449 A at 10 ns | +0.552 V | 0.396 | 32.01 |
| EY (REF_Y, SEL2, to P15V) | 32.9 mA at 0.483 ms | +0.140 V | 0.000 | 0.53 |
| EBP (BPF_OUT to P15V) | 0.1 mA | +0.049 V | 0.000 | -0.05 |
| ED1, ED2 (D1, D2 to P15V) | 0.7 mA, 0.8 mA | -0.003 V, -0.298 V | 0.000 | -0.05 |

The EX peak is at 10 ns, the end of the first rising edge of REF_X. The rail is at 0.298 V then. The current falls as the rail rises. The rail rises from 0.298 V to 3.238 V at 10 ms, so the rail capacitance takes 11.1 uF x 2.940 V = 32.6 uC. EX delivers 32.01 uC and EY 0.53 uC, so the charge balance closes within 0.1 uC. The EY peak of 32.9 mA is at the first REF_Y edge (T/4 = 0.483 ms). It is mostly the displacement current of CJO, because the diode voltage is only 0.14 V there. EBP, ED1 and ED2 reach 0.05 V or less, so they do not conduct forward. Their peaks are edge spikes and their charge is the reverse current -IS over 10 ms (5 uA x 10 ms = 0.05 uC). Only EX is a real forward conduction path.

The current-limiting element in this path is the 1 Ohm stand-in only. The reference source of the netlist is ideal. On the board the REF_X net connects only J7 pin 4, TP8 and U24 pin 1 (SEL1), and the REF_Y net only J7 pin 6, TP9 and U24 pin 9 (SEL2). There is no series resistor (KiCad netlist of commit 16ad253, exported 8 November 2026). The peak scales as (3.3 V minus the rail voltage minus the forward drop) divided by the series resistance, so the 2.45 A is a property of the 1 Ohm placeholder. The charge does not depend on the resistance. With a real ESD diode (forward drop about 0.6 V instead of about 0.05 V at the end of the charge) the rail would stop near 2.7 V instead of 3.24 V, and the charge would be about 30 uC.

## Discussion

**ESD path.** The entry assumed the references swing from 0 V to 3.3 V inside rails that are already at ±15 V. In this order the references are on at t = 0 and the +15 V rail is still at 0 V. REF_X then feeds the unpowered rail through the SEL1 stand-in. The rail rises to 3.2 V in 1.25 ms and stays there until the J4 ramp begins at 10 ms. The current into the rail is set by the stand-in resistance (1 Ohm, a placeholder) and the rail capacitance. The 11.1 uF of the circuit agrees with the board: C52 10 uF, C53 100 nF and C96 1 uF on the +15V net (and C54, C55, C95 with the same values on -15V). The peak of 2.45 A at the first edge follows from the 1 Ohm value. The charge of 32 uC is set by the capacitor, and it is the same for any series resistance. This is an effect of the board order, not a simulator artifact.

The real pin supports the stand-in on REF_X and REF_Y. SEL1 and SEL2 are digital inputs with a 6 MOhm pull-down and an absolute maximum of VDD + 0.3 V and ±30 mA (SCDS397B §5.1 and §5.5). The limit refers to VDD, so the pins are not tolerant of a high level while VDD is unpowered. A 3.3 V level with VDD = 0 V exceeds the rating by 3 V, and the input clamp to VDD conducts. On the board the current is limited only by the NUCLEO pin driver and the ribbon cable. The output resistance of the NUCLEO-F303RE pins was not read from the STM32F303 datasheet (not verified). For a driver of the order of 50 Ohm the first peak would be of the order of 50 mA, above the 30 mA rating, and the rail would charge with a time constant of about 0.5 ms.

**Back-powering before J5.** P5VA is 1.64 V before J5 is on. The ideal DAC80508 sources V_DAC2 and V_DAC3 (2.4 V) drive the +IN pins of U10A and U10B, and the input clamps of the OPAx196 model feed +5VA. The ±5 V op amps then run at a low supply and set BPF_OUT to 0.20 V to 0.41 V. EBP passes this to P15V at a diode voltage of 0.05 V. This is why P15V starts at 0.298 V at t = 0, while REF_X is still 0 V. On the board the DAC80508 is supplied from +5VA, so its outputs are 0 V before J5. This back-powering is a model artifact. It ends at the J5 ramp and does not change the results after the ramp.

The reference drive is not seen on the negative rail. M15V stays within 0.005 V of 0 V before 10 ms, because the negative stand-ins are reverse biased while the references are positive.

**Settling.** The fitted time constant of VSUP_A is R60 times C74 (1 kOhm times 10 uF = 10.0 ms). X_OUT and Y_OUT settle about 2.5 time constants of the post-filter after the ramp end. They do not settle over 10 time constants of the drain filter. The demodulator post-filter (R14 and R15 at 200 kOhm, C13 and C15 at 15 nF) is a unity-gain Sallen-Key filter with equal components. Its step response is critically damped, with a double pole at tau = 3 ms. Fitted as one exponential, the step response (1 + x) exp(-x), with x = t / tau, gives an apparent time constant of (1 + x) / x times tau. At x = 3 to 9 this is 3.0 ms to 4.0 ms, which matches the fitted values of 3.65 ms to 4.17 ms. Solving (1 + x) exp(-x) = 0.004 gives x of about 7.6, so 1 mV is reached about 23 ms after the post-filter starts to respond. The measured 1 mV times are 16 ms to 26 ms after the ramp end.

The 10 ms drain filter (R60 and C74) and the 10 ms gate filter (R8 and C8) do not set the X and Y settling. When X_OUT is within 1 mV of its final value, the boxcar mean of VSUP_A is still 86 mV (tramp = 1 ms) below its final value. VSUP_A reaches 1 mV only at 120 ms to 129 ms. The drain DC is removed by the high-pass in front of the demodulator, so a change of the drain DC reaches X_OUT only through small secondary effects. The band-pass poles (about 0.6 ms, from the equal-component K = 2 Sallen-Key stages at 518 Hz) are faster than the post-filter and do not dominate. The post-filter at tau = 3 ms dominates the settling of X_OUT and Y_OUT.

**Board value of the post-filter.** The board has cpf = 1.5 uF, so each pole has tau = 200 kOhm x 1.5 uF = 0.3 s. The post-filter then barely moves during the ramp and starts from about 0 V, so the deviation is the final value. Solving (1 + x) exp(-x) = 1 mV / 245 mV gives x = 7.66 for X_OUT, and 1 mV / 56 mV gives x = 5.97 for Y_OUT. With the board value X_OUT and Y_OUT settle within 1 mV about 2.3 s and 1.8 s after the J5 ramp. This fails the 250 ms limit of the entry, which holds only for the fast value cpf = 15 nF. A board-value run would need about 3 s of simulated time. Not simulated.

## Open points

1. **Stand-in model.** The DSCHOTTKY stand-in carries its reverse saturation current of -5 uA as a DC current (IS = 5 uA), and its 100 pF CJO gives edge spikes up to 33 mA. Both are artifacts. They are separated from forward conduction by the diode voltage, so the verdict of criterion 1 does not need a rerun. An area factor on each stand-in (for example `D_EX REF_X KEX DSCHOTTKY 1e-6`) scales IS and CJO down with one diode model, but it also scales RS up to 0.08 Ohm / 1e-6 = 80 kOhm. That acts as an 80 kOhm series resistor and changes the start-up result, so it is not a drop-in fix.
2. **Start-up peak on the board.** The 2.45 A peak uses the 1 Ohm placeholder. The NUCLEO-F303RE pin driver and the ribbon cable set the real peak. The output resistance was not read from the STM32F303 datasheet. The charge of 32 uC does not depend on them.
3. **Power sequencing (for the design report).** The sequence "NUCLEO references running before J4" is not safe as specified. SEL1 and SEL2 are rated to VDD + 0.3 V and ±30 mA (SCDS397B §5.1), and the board has no series resistor between J7 and U24. The board needs a series resistor at U24 pin 1 and pin 9, or a sequencing rule: the reference timers start only after J4 is on and stop before J4 is switched off (power-down was not simulated). A resistor of 3.3 kOhm limits the current to (3.3 V - 0.6 V) / 3.3 kOhm = 0.8 mA with VDD = 0 V. With the 6 MOhm pull-down it lowers the high level by 0.06 %, and with about 10 pF of pin and trace capacitance it adds about 33 ns of delay to both references. The SEL pin capacitance was not read from the datasheet (not verified). With the resistor the rail still rises to about 2.7 V if the references run long enough, but within the current rating.
4. **Other NUCLEO lines.** The same question applies to the NUCLEO lines into parts supplied from J5: SPI to the DAC80508, the MUX address and enable lines to the TMUX1109, and EXC_DAC into U1 through R6 = 120 kOhm. These are not covered by this entry and were not checked.
5. **TMUX6136 supply.** The SPDT_PREC model does not model its supply (`vendor/tmux6136/TMUX6136_BEH.LIB`). The switch outputs D1 and D2 follow the input (BPF_OUT or GND) and the reference control regardless of the rail voltage. The D1 and D2 results of this run are therefore not a test of the demodulator input during power-up. Power-down is not simulated, as the entry states.
6. **Ideal DAC80508 sources.** The bias DAC sources are on from t = 0. This is not physical before J5, because the DAC80508 is supplied from +5VA. They back-power +5VA to 1.64 V and set BPF_OUT to 0.2 V to 0.4 V before 50 ms (item 0 of the comparison). A board-like run would switch the DAC80508 sources on with J5.
7. **GATE.** The settling time of GATE was not fitted, because its deviation is within the 2f0 ripple. This does not affect the pass criteria.
8. **Diagnostic runs.** The failed and short runs (P31_test_v, P31_bisect_*) are in `sim-runs/`. Their results are not used. The quirks log has the causes.
