# P-25 Band-pass tolerance Monte Carlo (SIM-14 for the board)

## Goal

Find the gain and phase spread at 518 Hz of the K = 2 band-pass of LIA-AFE-Eval for the first build (C0G ±5 %, decision E-5) and for the ±1 % rework. Check each against SIM-14 (gain 4.00 ± 1 % and phase 0 ± 2 deg per band-pass).

## Runs

| Run | Circuit | Command | Status | Time | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P25a_mc5 | `circuits/afe_mc.cir` (C 5 %) | `python tools/run_sim.py circuits/afe_mc.cir --name P25a_mc5 --analysis ".AC LIN 1 518 518" --extra ".MC 500 AC VP([BPF_OUT]) YMAX OUTPUT ALL SEED=17" --extra ".OPTIONS NUMDGT=8" --probe "V(BPF_OUT)" --timeout 3600` | done, exit code 0 | 869 s | `sim-runs/P25a_mc5/P25a_mc5.csd` |
| P25b_mc1 | `circuits/afe_mc_P25b.cir` (C 1 %) | same, with `--name P25b_mc1` and the P25b circuit | done, exit code 0 | 746 s | `sim-runs/P25b_mc1/P25b_mc1.csd` |

Both `.out` files have no `ERROR` line and one `WARNING(ORPSIM-16534)` (high ITL4 may increase job time), which is harmless here. Both runs finished well inside the 3600 s timeout, so no server was left running.

The analysis script is `docs/sim-results/scripts/P-25_mc.py`. Run it from `simulations/ti-pspice` with `python docs/sim-results/scripts/P-25_mc.py sim-runs/P25a_mc5/P25a_mc5.csd sim-runs/P25b_mc1/P25b_mc1.csd`. It prints the PSpice statistics, the check against the entry table and the numpy yields of Table 3 (numpy with seed 1 and 200 000 samples per tolerance).

## Method

**Circuit.** `afe_mc.cir` is the front end of `afe_eval.cir`: INA828 U2 with R10, the K = 2 high-pass (C10, C11, R9, R13, U3A with R70 and R71) and the K = 2 low-pass (R11, R12, C9, C12, U3B with R72 and R73). The inputs are an ideal differential source with 1 V AC (`V_DP` 0.5 V AC on DRN_B, `V_DN` -0.5 V AC on DRN_A) at the drain DC level of 0.1575 V. The rails are ideal ±5.12 V. The OPAx196 and INA828 models are the vendor models of `nom_pspti.lib`, as in P-24.

**Tolerances.** The tolerances are literal in the models, `.MODEL RTOL RES(R=1 DEV=0.1%)` for R9, R10, R11, R12, R13, R70, R71, R72 and R73, and `.MODEL CTOL CAP(C=1 DEV=5%)` (P25a) or `DEV=1%` (P25b) for C9, C10, C11 and C12. The PSpice Reference Guide in the install (`C:\Cadence\PSpiceTI\doc\pspcref\01commands.html`, the `.OPTIONS DISTRIBUTION` entry) states that the default distribution is UNIFORM, with the deviation as a ± value. The numpy reference uses uniform distributions too.

**Measurement.** Section 0 of the CSDF is the Monte Carlo NOMINAL run. The other sections hold one AC point each at 518 Hz. The complex value H is read with `tools/csdf.py`. Gain error is `|H| / |H_nom| - 1` in percent. Phase deviation is `angle(H / H_nom)` in degrees. Both are relative to the nominal run of the same circuit. The statistics use `ddof = 1`. The standard errors come from a bootstrap of the samples with 2000 resamples.

**Sample count.** The `.out` files list `MONTE CARLO PASS 2` to `PASS 500`, so the nominal is pass 1 and the run has 499 random samples, not 500. This is the same as P-14 (`docs/sim-results/P-14.md`). The entry says 500 samples. The statistics are not affected by one sample, and the difference is noted in the open points.

**Nominal.** The nominal value at 518 Hz is 39.5778 V/V at -0.144 deg in both runs. The ideal front end is 39.587 V/V at +0.014 deg (`afe_eval_expected.py`). The vendor models give -0.023 % in gain and -0.158 deg in phase against the ideal. The P-24 value at 518 Hz, interpolated from its grid, is -0.1768 deg, so the difference here is 0.019 deg (-0.1583 deg against -0.1768 deg). The gain difference is 0.0007 %. Both are unexplained (not verified).

The P-24 front end is V(BPF_OUT) / (V(DRN_B) - V(DRN_A)) at the INA828 pins, so the source impedance of the drains cancels. Three causes are ruled out by arithmetic. The frequency is not the cause, because the P-24a deficit interpolated from 517.571 Hz to 518 Hz gives -0.1768 deg, against -0.1767 deg. The single-ended drive is not the cause, because the common-mode ratio is -0.50 and the INA828 model CMRR of about 118.8 dB (from P29_nom, |Gcm| = 4.57e-5 V/V) gives an error of about 3e-5 deg. The rails are not the cause, because P-24a reads 5.1197 V and -5.1311 V against ±5.12 V, with no first-order AC effect.

The only remaining structural difference is the BPF_OUT load in `afe_eval.cir`: R78 and R83 (10 kOhm each, to the virtual grounds) and the two S2 capacitances of the TMUX6136 (4 pF each). This load is absent in `afe_mc.cir`. A linear estimate with a closed-loop output impedance of about 0.04 Ohm gives less than 0.001 deg, so even this load does not explain the 0.019 deg (not verified). The test is to add R78, R83 and the switch capacitances to a copy of `afe_mc.cir` and rerun the nominal point.

**SIM-14 interpretation.** SIM-14 gives the band-pass gain as 4.00 V/V. That is the front-end gain divided by the INA828 gain of 9.8968 V/V. The relative gain error of this run is the same as the band-pass error, plus the 0.1 % tolerance of R10, which changes the INA828 gain by a very small amount. The phase is taken relative to the nominal phase in Tables 1 and 3. Table 3 also gives the yields with the absolute nominal phase of -0.144 deg included, because the limit of SIM-14 is 0 deg.

## Open points

1. **Gain yield of the 1 % build.** The entry sentence, which said the 1 % rework fails the gain limit on 2.5 % of boards, has been corrected: about half of the 1 % boards fail the gain limit. PSpice and numpy agree on this (48.1 % and 47.5 % inside ±1 %). The 1 % rework does not meet the SIM-14 gain limit for all boards without a per-board calibration or a tighter gain tolerance.
2. **Sample count.** The `.MC 500` line gives 499 random samples plus the nominal (pass 1), as in P-14. The entry says 500 samples. The difference does not change the statistics.
3. **Absolute phase.** The phase limit of SIM-14 is 0 deg, and the nominal phase of the vendor model is -0.144 deg. Including this nominal phase raises the 5 % phase yield from 44.1 % to 44.7 % and the both-limits yield from 2.8 % to 3.0 %. The 1 % yields stay at 99.8 % and 47.9 %.
4. **Distribution.** The PSpice Reference Guide in the install (`C:\Cadence\PSpiceTI\doc\pspcref\01commands.html`, `.OPTIONS DISTRIBUTION`) gives the default as UNIFORM, with the deviation as a ± value. The echoed options in both `.out` files contain no DISTRIBUTION line, so the default is in effect. GAUSS would treat the value as 1 sigma and give a std about 1.73 times the uniform std (a uniform ±a has std a / sqrt(3)). The data exclude this, because the 5 % std of 6.999 % matches the uniform numpy value of 7.13 %.
5. **Vendor nominal.** The nominal phase of -0.1583 deg against the ideal differs from the P-24 value of -0.1768 deg at 518 Hz by 0.019 deg, and the gain differs by 0.0007 %. The cause is not explained. The ruled-out causes are listed in the Method section. The only remaining structural difference is the BPF_OUT load of `afe_eval.cir` (R78, R83 and the two switch capacitances of 4 pF). A linear estimate gives less than 0.001 deg, so this is not verified. The test is to add these parts to a copy of `afe_mc.cir`.
6. **Calibration.** The spread is calibrated out per board in the design (decision E-5 and the entry text). The 1 % build gives a std of 1.41 % gain and 0.68 deg phase. Whether a per-board calibration is used is a design decision outside this entry.
