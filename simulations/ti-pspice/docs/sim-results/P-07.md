# P-07 Frequency mismatch

## Goal

Show the output when the input frequency differs slightly from the reference.

## Method

**Expected output.** The input is 2.5 V + 2 V sin(2 pi x 518 Hz x t). Each reference is a 50 % square wave at 517 Hz. The demodulator multiplies the input by the reference sign. The odd harmonic n of the reference (amplitude 4/(n pi)) gives two products with the input, at (518 - 517 n) Hz and (518 + 517 n) Hz. Each product has amplitude 2A/(n pi), with A = 2 V.

For n = 1 the difference product is the 1 Hz beat. Its amplitude is K = 2A/pi = 1.273240 V. The RC filter multiplies it by

|H(1 Hz)| = 1 / sqrt(1 + (2 pi x 1 Hz x 0.1 s)^2) = 0.846733

which gives an amplitude of 1.078094 V and a phase lag of atan(2 pi x 0.1) = 32.142 deg. The Y reference delay of 482.6 us adds the phase theta = 360 deg x 517 Hz x 482.6 us = 89.82 deg, so the steady state is

- VX = 2.5 V + 1.078094 V cos(2 pi t + 147.858 deg) + ripple
- VY = 2.5 V + 1.078094 V cos(2 pi t - 122.320 deg) + ripple

The X minus Y phase is -89.82 deg, which is 90 deg minus d with d = 0.18 deg.

**Start transient.** The filter is first order and linear, and its input has the form 2.5 V plus sinusoids. The exact solution from V(0) = 0 V is

y(t) = y_ss(t) - y_ss(0) exp(-t / tau)

where y_ss(t) is the steady state. At t = 0, y_ss(0) = 2.5 V + Re(sum of the phasors). This gives y_ss(0) = 1.58714 V for VX and 1.92136 V for VY, so the transient amplitudes are -1.58714 V and -1.92136 V. The transient decays with tau = 0.1 s and is gone at 1 s (exp(-10) = 4.5e-5).

**Ripple.** The products at 1035 Hz (n = 1, sum) and 1033 Hz (n = 3, difference) are the 2 f terms. They are not one line at 1034 Hz. With fin = 518 Hz they are 1 Hz apart. Higher products lie at 2067 Hz, 2069 Hz, 3101 Hz and above. The P-06 value of 1.3 mV for X at 1034 Hz belongs to fin = 517 Hz, where both products coincide.

**Calibrated model.** The index model is ideal. The P-02 run measured a common gain deficit of -0.039 % and constant delays of 127 ns (X) and 136 ns (Y). The calibrated model uses these values. It is shown next to the ideal model.

**Fits.** Each fit uses the window 1 s to 3 s, with the data on a uniform 5 us grid. The CSDF points are linearly interpolated to this grid.

- Fit A is the brief's fit, c + A cos(2 pi f_b t + phi), with f_b free (`scipy.optimize.curve_fit`).
- Fit B is a linear least squares fit with DC, the 1 Hz beat, all products at integer frequencies up to 8273 Hz, and the transient exp(-t/tau). All frequencies are integer over the 2 s window, so the products are orthogonal and the fit is exact for the model.

The script is `docs/sim-results/scripts/P-07_frequency_mismatch.py`. Run it from the project root with `python docs/sim-results/scripts/P-07_frequency_mismatch.py`. It needs numpy, scipy and matplotlib, and it reads the `.csd` file directly.

| Channel | Line | Expected (mV) | Fit (mV) | Ratio |

The 1035 Hz line is 1.956 mV in both channels. The two lines beat with each other at 2 Hz, so the peak-to-peak ripple of the 2 f content is up to 2 x (1.958 + 0.654) mV = 5.2 mV. This is ripple on the 1 Hz beat, and it has no DC effect over the 2 s window.

### 5. Amplitude error and the time step

The beat amplitude error against the ideal model is -0.041 % for VX and -0.043 % for VY. The beat fit is nearly free of settling bias, because the transient is about 0.07 mV at 1 s and decays with tau = 0.1 s.

The 20 us step affects the gain at 517 Hz. The settled outputs of two existing runs at Vampl = 2 V give a direct test. Both are raw 50-period means over 0.903288 s to 1.000000 s, without the settling correction, so the settling correction cancels in the difference.

| Run | Maximum step | Settled VX (V) | Amplitude 2.5 V - VX (V) | Deficit against K (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P04_ampl, 2 V section | 20 us | 1.2273216 | 1.2726784 | -0.044 |
| P04_step5u | 5 us | 1.2268980 | 1.2731020 | -0.011 |

The change from 20 us to 5 us is 0.033 %. The formula (n x 2 pi f_osc x h)^2 / 12 from the P-05 judge note gives 0.035 % at h = 20 us. The two values agree.

## Discussion

**The data follow the expected waveform.** The beat frequency, the X minus Y phase, the beat amplitude and the 1035 Hz and 1033 Hz ripple all agree with the model. The residual after fit B is 0.047 mV RMS, which is set by the interpolation and the sampling.

**The step-size error is visible.** The 1 Hz beat amplitude is 0.44 mV (0.041 %) below the ideal model. The statistical error of the fit is about 1e-4 mV, so the deficit is far above the noise. The 20 us and 5 us runs at Vampl = 2 V show a 0.033 % change in the 517 Hz output, which matches the 0.035 % from the P-05 formula. The 20 us deficit of the 517 Hz output (-0.044 %) is close to the beat deficit (-0.041 %). The two values agree within 0.003 %. The beat is generated by the same switching, so the step error should be the same. This is an assumption, and a 5 us run of this circuit would test it. That run was not made here.

**Phase offset.** The beat phases are 147.90 deg (X) and -122.28 deg (Y). The ideal values are 147.86 deg and -122.32 deg. Both channels are about 0.04 deg from the ideal model. A phase offset of 0.02 deg on a 1.078 V beat gives 0.32 mV, which is the 1 Hz residual of the calibrated model. The P-02 delays change the calibrated phase by only 0.02 deg, so the rest of the offset is not explained. A common offset of this size is consistent with the reference edges and the switch delays. This is not tested here.

**The ripple lines.** The 1035 Hz line is 0.11 % low. The linear interpolation of the 20 us output points lowers a 1035 Hz sine by about (omega h)^2 / 8 = 0.21 %, with omega h = 0.130. The measured ripple ratio is therefore not a clean test of the step error. The 1 Hz beat is not affected, because omega h is 1.3e-4 at 1 Hz.

**The transient.** The start transient matches the exact solution within 0.7 mV. The 1 s to 3 s fit cannot determine it, which is expected.
