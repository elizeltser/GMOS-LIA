## Method

**Averaging.** Each mean is a time-weighted trapezoid mean over 20 whole periods of f0 = 518 Hz (one period 1930.502 us, window 38.610 ms) from 60 ms to 98.61 ms. The point mean is not used, because the adaptive step biases it (`docs/simulation-quirks.md`).

**Sampling and breakpoints.** The largest sample step inside the window is 2.02 us in every run (2.018 us in P28a). The maximum step of the `.TRAN` line is 2 us. Around one REF_X edge the samples fall at 0.0, 1.0, 3.0, 3.5, 4.5, 6.5, 10.0, 10.4, 11.2, 12.8 and 16.0 ns after the edge (script, P28d at 2 V). Samples therefore resolve the PULSE edges, which PSpice places as breakpoints.

**Settling.** The entry compares the window with the 20 periods before it (21.39 ms to 60 ms). The filter is two equal poles with R14 C13 = 200 kOhm x 15 nF = 3 ms. That reference window still holds the start-up transient, so the entry's comparison measures the transient. Three checks are reported. (1) The analytic mean deficit of the reference window, 5.680e-4 of the final value, is compared with the measured difference. (2) A half-window check compares the second 10 periods (79.31 ms to 98.61 ms) with the first 10 periods (60 ms to 79.31 ms). (3) The analytic residual of the 60 ms window is (1 + t/tau) exp(-t/tau) = 4.3e-8 of the final value.

**Zero-signal offset.** The run P28c gives the X_OUT and Y_OUT offset at vos_in = 0, X0 = Y0 = 0.04758 mV. The offset-corrected gain error is (X - X0 - ideal) / ideal. As a second estimate, the intercept of a linear fit of the raw X error against ain (0.1 V to 3 V) is 0.047531 mV for P28a. The P28b correction uses its own regression intercept, 0.048290 mV, because no zero-signal run exists at 5.0 V. The P28a intercept validates the method: it differs from X0 by 0.05 uV.

**Ideal values.** X_OUT = (2/pi) ain cos(p) and Y_OUT = -(2/pi) ain sin(p), the convention of the Batch 4 section.

**Edge model.** The Y error at p = 0 (and the X error at p = 90) is modelled by three terms, with MAG the magnitude of the offset-corrected error:

MAG = 4 f0 delta A + 4 f0 A^2 / SR - c

delta is the break-before-make lag of the switch, SR is the rising slew rate of the output, and c is a recovery term that is independent of the amplitude. Both reference edges of each period step the op-amp output from -A to +A (rising), so only the rising rate enters at p = 0 (Y) and p = 90 (X). A reference lag gives (2/pi) A cos(p + omega delta), so the errors are negative. The three terms act in sequence on each edge. X_DEM moves less than 1 mV on average until D1 crosses 50 %, then it slews. The slew-only fit below therefore absorbs the delay and recovery terms.

**Transition region.** The entry uses the OPA2196 input transition region of V+ - 3 V to V+ - 1.5 V, with V+ = vpa. This gives 1.60 V to 3.10 V at 4.6 V and 2.00 V to 3.50 V at 5.0 V. The input D1 is 0 V in the -1 state and equal to BPF_OUT in the +1 state. Its peak over the settled window is compared with the region. The model has no common-mode-dependent offset step, so the transition region is not tested by this simulation (see Discussion).

**Clipping.** The first ain where the offset-corrected gain error exceeds 1 %.

## Discussion

**Linearity.** After the zero-signal offset is removed, the gain error is -0.00065 % from 0.4 V to 3 V in both runs. The error at 10 mV is -0.0012 % (4.6 V) and -0.0005 % (5.0 V). The raw error below 0.1 V is the offset of 0.048 mV, which is 0.75 % at 10 mV. The entry should state whether linearity is judged with or without the zero-signal offset. This summary judges it with the offset removed, because the offset is judged separately in P28c.

**Transition region: not tested.** The OPAx196 model in the simulation has no common-mode-dependent offset step. The model clamps the common-mode voltage at the rails and has a linear CMRR and PSRR. The absence of any change in X_OUT at 2 V to 3 V in the simulation is therefore a property of the model, not evidence about the hardware. The datasheet (SBOS869 page 24, section 7.3.6, text layer) states that the transition region is typically V+ - 3 V to V+ - 1.5 V and that offset, offset drift, CMRR and PSRR vary within it. The offset-versus-common-mode figure is an image in the PDF, and its values cannot be read from the text layer. The table on page 7 gives Vos of ±100 uV maximum at V+ - 1.5 V and refers to the typical-characteristics figure inside the region. No hand bound for the Vos change inside the region is therefore available from the datasheet text. The transition-region error needs a bench check of U26A at 2 V to 3 V peak input, or a model that includes the Vos change. The OPAx196 model file was not found on disk, so the model line numbers cited in the first grading were not re-read.

**Edge errors.** The Y error at p = 0 and the X error at p = 90 are the same error seen in two channels. The three-term model explains it at 1 V to 3 V within 7.7 uV, with the constants quoted above. The delta term dominates below 0.4 V. The slew term dominates above 1.5 V. At 0.1 V and 0.4 V the recovery term of the model is larger than the measured error, so the amplitude dependence of the recovery term is not resolved. This is an open point.

**Edge spikes.** The D1 and X_DEM spikes at the switch edges have two parts. The first is a break excursion of about ±9 mV on D1 during the break between the old and the new switch state. It matches the injected charge of the switch on the floating node (script: 9.07 mV and 9.08 mV). The second is a post-closure overshoot, which peaks 45 ns to 58 ns after the edge. Its size varies from edge to edge. The overshoot does not change the mean output beyond the edge error above.

**Slew rate.** The fitted empirical slew rate of 7.60 V/us over 1.5 V to 3 V and the three-term fit of 8.00 V/us are not datasheet values. The datasheet rising rate at ±18 V is 7.5 V/us (page 8). The falling rate of 5.5 V/us does not enter at p = 0 or p = 90, because both reference edges of each period step the output upwards (script: all X_DEM edges have a positive slope).

**Input bias current.** The datasheet gives IB of ±5 pA typical and ±20 pA maximum (page 7). The current through R14 + R15 of 5.08 pA in the bias table lies inside these limits.

**Zero-signal offset.** The offset of 47.5 uV on X_OUT and Y_OUT is the sum of the input offset of U26B (16.5 uV) and twice the input offset of U26A (33 uV), less the input current of U26B through 400 kOhm (2.0 uV). The bias point and the transient mean agree to 0.04 uV at vos_in = 0. The settling of P28c (about 50 uV at ±0.1 V DC) is the start-up of the post-filter with REF_X and REF_Y low at the bias point, as shown in the table above.

**Settling.** The 60 ms window is settled to 0.03 uV at 1 V. The entry's 20-period comparison is dominated by the reference window. The excess of 0.45 % over the analytic transient is a small unexplained term. The Y half-window differences of -3.5 uV to +4.4 uV at 2 V to 2.5 V are edge-to-edge scatter. The first grading estimates the scatter of the half-window difference from the per-edge area (standard deviation 8.9 V ns at 2 V). This estimate gives about 2.9 uV, which agrees with the observed range. The estimate is not reproduced by the script, and it is marked as an estimate. The mean of Y is uncertain by about 1.5 uV from the same scatter.

## Open points

1. The transition region (rows 9 and 10) is not tested by the model. A bench check or a model with a common-mode-dependent offset is needed. The datasheet figure on page 24 of SBOS869 gives the values, which are not available as text.
2. The recovery term c of the three-term model is not resolved at low amplitude. At 0.1 V and 0.4 V the model overpredicts the error by 106 uV and 68 uV. A run with the reference edges moved off the peak (for example p = 45 deg) would test the amplitude dependence. The three constants are correlated, so a separate fit with a fixed delta would also help.
3. The fitted slew rate of 7.60 V/us (empirical) and 8.00 V/us (three-term) are not datasheet values. The model value at ±5 V is not read.
4. The regression intercepts differ slightly from the values of the first grading: P28a 0.047531 mV (0.047548 mV quoted) and P28b 0.048290 mV (0.04832 mV quoted). The difference of 0.03 uV changes the P28b 10 mV value from -0.0005 % to -0.0003 %, which passes in both cases.
5. The bias point gives X_DEM of 33.0586 uV, while 2 x 16.5147 uV = 33.0294 uV. The difference of 0.029 uV is the input current of U26A at the -IN node (2.9 pA through R79 = 10 kOhm, from KCL at U26AM). The U26B current of 5.08 pA from the bias table differs from the 5.25 pA of the first grading.
6. The DC Vos of the model (13.6 uV in the first grading) and the cited OPAx196 model line numbers were not verified, because the model file was not found on disk.
7. The Y half-window differences (-3.5 uV to +4.4 uV) are edge scatter. The estimate of 2.9 uV from the per-edge area is not reproduced by the script.
8. The excess of 0.45 % of the entry's settling comparison (1.6 uV at 1 V, 4.9 uV at 3 V) is a small unexplained term.
9. No zero-signal run exists at 5.0 V. The P28b offset is taken from the regression intercept. A P28c-style run at 5.0 V would confirm it.
10. The D1 spike sizes of the first grading (0.50 to 9.96 mV at 0.4 V and 0.79 to 19.68 mV at 2 V) use a definition not in the script. The script's definition gives per-edge overshoot sizes of -18.1 to +9.5 mV at 0.4 V and -136.5 to +20.4 mV at 2 V, measured against the post-edge level. The break excursion of ±9 mV matches the injected charge on the floating node.
11. The entry's description "X_DEM swings ±ain" is wrong at p = 0. X_DEM is rectified (0 to +ain). The owner should correct the entry. No change was made to the entry.
12. The `R` in the P28d bound is undefined. This summary reads it as (2/pi) ain.
13. The circuit copies `circuits/afe_demod_P28b.cir`, `circuits/afe_demod_P28c.cir` and `circuits/afe_demod_P28d.cir` are new and not yet tracked by git.
