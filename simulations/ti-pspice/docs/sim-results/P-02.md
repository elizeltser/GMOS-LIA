# P-02 Input phase sweep

## Goal

Verify that X and Y respond as cos and sin of the input phase over a full turn, and that the magnitude is independent of phase.

## Method

**Settled values.** The settled value of each channel is the mean over the last 50 whole periods of 517 Hz (96.71 ms). The raw CSDF points are interpolated onto a uniform 1 us grid, and the trapezoidal mean is taken on that grid. This is the method of P-01.

**Settling check.** The same mean is taken over the 50 periods before the last window. The difference between the two windows is the settling check.

**Settling-bias correction.** The `.IC` start at 2.5 V leaves a decaying tail. The last-50-period mean is biased toward 2.5 V. The correction from P-01 is 0.097 mV at |V - 2.5 V| = 1.27 V, and it scales linearly with |V - 2.5 V|. It moves the mean toward the final value:

corrected = measured + 7.6e-5 x (measured - 2.5 V)

This gives at most 0.097 mV for any step. It is applied to VX and VY at every step.

**Ideal theory.** VX - 2.5 V = -K cos p and VY - 2.5 V = K sin(p - d), with K = 2A/pi = 1.273240 V and d = 0.17849 deg (P-01).

**Phase.** The measured phase is atan2(VY - 2.5 V, 2.5 V - VX). The ideal phase has the same formula applied to the ideal values. Because atan2 of the ideal values is not exactly p - d, the phase error is taken against the ideal atan2 value. The error against the line p - d is also reported.

**Fits.** The fits use the 24 distinct phases from 0 deg to 345 deg. The point at 360 deg repeats 0 deg. It is used only as a repeatability check. Two models are fitted to each channel:

- Base model: offset, cos p and sin p. This is the model of the brief.
- Extended model: the base model plus one slew term, sin p |sin p| for VX and cos p |cos p| for VY. The reason is given in the Discussion.

The scripts are `docs/sim-results/scripts/P-02_phase.py`, which reads the cache `sim-runs/P02_phase/P02_steps.csv`. The cache is written once from the `.csd` file. Run the script from the project root:

`python docs/sim-results/scripts/P-02_phase.py`

### Magnitude R

The ideal theory itself has a magnitude that depends on p. Its value is K sqrt(cos^2 p + sin^2(p - d)). It ranges from 1.271255 V to 1.275221 V, which is plus or minus 0.16 % about K. This comes from d. The check in the brief compares R with K, so it includes this variation. The column "R err vs ideal" removes it.

Against K, R lies between -0.195 % and +0.117 %. Against the ideal theory at the same p, R lies between -0.047 % and -0.033 %, with a standard deviation of 0.005 %. The common gain deficit is therefore -0.039 % in both channels, and it does not depend on p.

## Discussion

### The magnitude is independent of phase

Against the ideal theory, R is -0.039 % below K at every phase, with a spread of 0.005 %. This is the expected result for an independent magnitude. The fitted Rx and Ry are both 1.272742 V, so X and Y share the same gain deficit.

P-01 found a VX deficit of 0.036 % at p = 0. P-02 shows the same deficit in VY and at all phases. The deficit is therefore a common gain of the two demodulators or of the shared input path, not a fault of the X channel alone. The cause is not identified. Candidates include the finite open-loop gain of the OPAx192 model and the on-resistance of the switches. Neither has been quantified for this circuit.

### The phase follows d minus the Y delay, not d plus it

The brief expects the Y phase to be d plus about 0.044 deg. P-01 and this data give the opposite sign. A delay in the Y switching makes the Y reference act later. A later reference reduces the lag of VY. The P-01 Y effective delay of 234.7 ns therefore gives a phase of

phy = d - 360 deg x 517 Hz x 234.7 ns = 0.1785 deg - 0.0437 deg = 0.135 deg.

At p = 0 the measured phase is -0.135 deg. This matches d - 0.044 deg to 0.001 deg. The brief's sign would give 0.222 deg, which the data do not show. The brief should be read with the sign of P-01.

The phase error against the ideal atan2 value is +0.038 deg to +0.044 deg at all phases. This phase error is small and nearly constant, as expected for a fixed delay. The judge note of P-02 expected a variation of a few hundredths of a degree with p. The measured variation is 0.006 deg, which is at the low end of that expectation.

### Part of the delay is common to X and Y

The reference edges of both channels have a 50 % rise point 50 ns after the nominal edge, and a 50 % fall point 150 ns after the nominal fall (P-01). The mean offset of 100 ns is shared by both channels, so it shifts the phase of both channels. The switch changes add about 27 ns (mean of the X edges) and 36 ns (mean of the Y edges) on top of that.

The extended fit gives the constant parts directly:

| Channel | Fitted constant phase (deg) | Predicted constant phase (deg) | Difference (deg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| X | -0.023 | -0.024, from 100 ns + 27 ns = 127 ns | +0.001 |
| Y | +0.156 | +0.153, from d minus omega x (100 ns + 36 ns) | +0.003 |

The predicted X value uses the mean X switch change of 26.6 ns from the four X edges in P-01. The predicted Y value uses the mean Y switch change of 35.6 ns from P-01. The differences are 0.001 deg (about 4 ns) and 0.003 deg (about 16 ns). Both are small. The 16 ns gap is not explained.

### The residual pattern shows a p-dependent delay

The residual after the offset, cos and sin terms has a clear p-dependence. VX has a residual of the form S_x sin p abs(sin p). VY has a residual of the form S_y cos p abs(cos p). These are the shapes of the slew-limited delay. The demodulator output has a step of about 4 abs(sin p) V on X and 4 abs(cos p) V on Y at the switching instant. A step of this size takes time D = step / SR to complete. The mean sees a delay of D/2.

The slew rate of the OPAx192 model is 20.19 V/us (P-01). At p = 0 the Y delay is 99.1 ns, which gives a VY shift of 0.410 mV. The fitted coefficients are:

| Channel | Shape | Fitted S (mV) | Predicted S (mV) | Ratio | Equivalent delay (ns) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| X | sin p abs(sin p) | +0.477 | +0.410 | 1.16 | 115 |
| Y | cos p abs(cos p) | +0.480 | +0.410 | 1.17 | 115 |

Both coefficients have the same sign and the same size. This supports one common slew mechanism with the same slew rate in both demodulators. The slew term reduces the RMS residual after the base fit from 0.059 mV to 0.013 mV on VX and 0.011 mV on VY. This removes about 95 % of the residual variance of VX and about 97 % of that of VY.

The fitted slew coefficients are 16 % to 17 % higher than the P-01 estimate. The equivalent delay is 115 ns instead of 99 ns. The data do not show which part of the output edge causes the difference. A slower start of the slew, below 50 % of the step, is one candidate. This is not verified.

### Why the slew term changes R only a little

The slew term adds to VX and VY in directions that cancel at first order in the magnitude at p = 45 deg and at 135 deg. Its effect on R is therefore small. The per-step R error against K has a sin 2p pattern. The ideal theory has the same pattern from d (Magnitude R section). Against the ideal theory, the pattern is removed, and the residual spread is 0.005 %.

## Open points

1. The common gain deficit of -0.039 % in X and Y is not explained. A run at A = 0 V separates an offset from a gain error, as P-01 open point 2 proposed. A run at A = 1 V gives the scaling with amplitude.
2. The fitted slew coefficient is 17 % above the P-01 prediction. The cause is not identified. A probe on the demodulator output nodes around the Y edge at p = 90 deg would show the full edge shape.
3. The Y constant phase differs from its prediction by 0.003 deg (about 16 ns). Not explained.
4. The analysis brief for P-02 gave the Y phase as d + 0.044 deg. P-01 and this data give d - 0.044 deg. The index entry itself states only d, so it is not affected. The brief sign should not be used for later phase checks.
5. The warnings ORPSIM-15459 (default TR and TF) and ORPSIM-15256 (VTH not a subcircuit parameter) appear in the log. Both are explained as harmless in `docs/simulation-quirks.md` (the edges were measured at 80 ns 10 to 90 % in P01_D_edges).
