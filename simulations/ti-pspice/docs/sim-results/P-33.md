## Method

The copies differ from `circuits/afe_eval.cir` only in the named lines. The P33a copies change `rhtr` (300, 600 and 1200 Ohm). The P33b copy replaces the DAC1 source by a pulse from 1.5 V to 1.6 V at 0.5 ms. The P33d copy adds `AC 1` to V_DAC2 for the transfer and noise input.

Analysis scripts are in `docs/sim-results/scripts/`: `P-33_heater.py` (P33a, P33b, headroom and settling checks), `P-33_drain.py` (P33c) and `P-33_noise.py` (P33d).

For P33a, the heater current is V(HPIX_A) / rhtr. The ideal feedback gain is 2, so the ideal HFB_A is 2 x vh_a. The limit is the last sweep point before the first point where |HFB_A - 2 vh_a| exceeds 1 mV. The HSENSE_B clip is at 3.3 V, which corresponds to 25 x 15 Ohm x I = 3.3 V, or I = 8.8 mA.

Headroom of U9A is split into three quantities. The available headroom is P5VP minus V(HSH_A). It is not a design margin, because it is the output voltage left below the rail. The needed headroom is the swing that the OPAx196 model requires at the output current I_out, 10.9 mV + 40.8 mV/mA x I_out. I_out is the shunt current (HSH_A - HFB_A) / 15 Ohm, which is the heater current plus the 0.04 mA of the feedback divider R20 + R22 (99.8 kOhm). It comes from the output swing curve of the CLAWP_OPAx196 subcircuit in OPAx196.LIB (via R72 = 1 kOhm), the TI model fit, which was not checked against the datasheet. The net headroom is the available headroom minus the needed headroom. A net headroom near zero means that the output stage sits on the swing curve and saturates. The HFB_A ceiling is (P5VP - 0.0109 V) / (1 + (15 + 40.8) Ohm / R_eff), with R_eff = (rhtr + 4.5 Ohm) in parallel with 99.8 kOhm (heater, TMUX1109 on-resistance and feedback divider). It is the HFB_A value at which the net headroom reaches zero for the P5VP of the run. The short-circuit limits of the model (+70.2 mA and -65 mA) are far above the 14 mA of the 300 Ohm heater, so they do not limit the output here.

For P33b, the step at HFB_A is 0.2 V (the DAC step of 0.1 V times the gain 2). The final value is the mean of the last 0.2 ms (1.8 ms to 2.0 ms). The overshoot is (peak minus final) divided by the step, in per cent. The ringing frequency is 1 / (2 x mean spacing) of the first four extrema of the residual larger than 0.1 % of the step. The 50 ns maximum step gives about 160 time points per period of the 122.4 kHz ringing at 10 nF, so the ringing is resolved.

For P33c, the expected drain bias is VSUP_A = 1.1 x vdrc_a - 7.5 mV. The linearity is measured on the window vdrc_a = 0.46 V to 3.19 V (the window of the entry). The sweep step is 0.05 V, not the 0.01 V of the sweep range in the index entry (see Open points).

For P33d, the transfer from V_DAC2 to the node is the complex AC value at 518 Hz. The DAC noise is 100 nV/sqrt(Hz) at V_DAC2, applied as an ideal extra source. The device noise is the TOTAL OUTPUT NOISE VOLTAGE of the `.NOISE` table. The total is the root sum of squares.

### P-33a HSENSE_B clipping

The HSENSE_B clip starts between vh_a = 1.30 V and 1.35 V on the 0.05 V sweep grid. The clip current is therefore between 8.7 mA and 8.9 mA. The exact threshold is 8.80 mA, from 3.3 V / (25 x 15 Ohm). This agrees with finding F-09 of the 2026-10-29 review (clip above 8.8 mA).

### P-33b heater step response (1.5 V to 1.6 V on DAC1, 0.2 V on HFB_A)

| C_HTRA (nF) | Step at HFB_A (mV) | Peak (V) | Overshoot (%) | Settling to 1 % (us) | Ringing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 200.0 | 3.2036 | 1.78 | 2.18 | one undershoot of 1.48 %, then none above 0.1 % |
| 1 | 200.0 | 3.2451 | 22.51 | 4.63 | 370 kHz, decaying (+22.5 %, -4.9 %, +1.1 %, -0.2 %) |
| 10 | 200.0 | 3.3169 | 58.44 | 33.68 | 122.4 kHz, decaying (+58.4 %, -33.7 %, +19.4 %, -11.2 %) |

A first-order check of the 10 nF step. A second-order fit to the overshoot and to the decay of the residual peaks (58.4 % to 19.4 % in one period, logarithmic decrement 1.10) gives a damping ratio zeta = 0.17 and a natural frequency f_n = 124.5 kHz. The second-order overshoot exp(-pi zeta / sqrt(1 - zeta^2)) is then 58 %, which agrees with the 58.4 % simulated. The damped frequency f_n sqrt(1 - zeta^2) is 122.7 kHz, against 122.4 kHz simulated. The 1 nF case gives zeta of about 0.5 and about 16 % overshoot. The simulated 22.5 % is higher, so a second pole of the op amp adds to the overshoot. The fit corresponds to an effective series resistance R_s of about 1.45 kOhm.

## Discussion

The heater driver holds the commanded heater voltage up to 4.60 V on the 600 Ohm and 1.2 kOhm heaters. The 600 Ohm heater has only about 44 mV of net headroom at that point, so the output stage is close to the swing curve. The 1200 Ohm heater has about 271 mV. The available voltage P5VP minus HSH_A is not headroom by itself, because the output stage needs part of it to supply the heater current.

The 300 Ohm heater is limited by the output swing of the OPAx196 model. At 14 mA the model needs about 0.58 V below V+, and the output stage cannot supply more. The ceiling from the swing curve is 4.267 V, and the simulation reaches 4.275 V, 8 mV higher, in line with the -9 mV net headroom. The curve is the TI model fit, and it was not checked against the datasheet. The HSENSE_B clip near 8.8 mA agrees with F-09, so the sensing limit is confirmed for this heater.

The heater step response depends strongly on the load capacitance. At 100 pF the response is well damped, with 1.8 % overshoot. At 1 nF the overshoot is 22.5 % with a decaying ring at 370 kHz. At 10 nF the loop rings at 122.4 kHz with 58 % overshoot. The ringing decays in both cases, so the loop is stable but poorly damped at 1 nF and 10 nF. A first-order model of the output stage explains most of this. The model output impedance is about 1.47 kOhm and resistive from about 2 Hz to 450 kHz. It comes from the output stage (RX 330 kOhm, RDUMMY 33 kOhm, local loop gain about 214). With the feedback factor 0.5 and the 15 Ohm shunt, this impedance forms a pole with the load capacitance and the parallel of R_L and R_s. The 10 nF fit gives zeta = 0.17, which is about 19 deg of phase margin, and needs an effective R_s of about 1.45 kOhm. The 1 nF case gives about 16 % overshoot in the same model, against 22.5 % simulated, and the difference is the second pole of the op amp. An isolation resistor adds to the 1.47 kOhm of the output stage, so the size of a fix needs a simulation that is not part of this entry. The values of chtr are a sweep and not a known cable or heater capacitance.

The drain bias amplifier follows its expected formula within 0.02 mV over the whole window. The OPAx196 model shows no step at its transition, as stated for P-28, so the linearity result is a model property. The pixel stand-in is an ideal 7.5 uA sink, and R_R25 carries 7.5002 uA even where DRN_A is below 0 V. The linearity of VSUP_A and the 1.1 vdrc_a - 7.5 mV result therefore hold over the whole window. The question for the owner is the compliance of the real pixel (GMOS model TBD). A drop of 7.5 uA across 330 kOhm is 2.475 V, so VSUP_A must stay above about 2.5 V plus the pixel compliance for the pixel to sink its current. The 0.5 V to 3.5 V window of the entry is then inconsistent with that drop below about 2.5 V.

The noise at VSUP_A is set by the DAC noise, which is 3.38 nV/sqrt(Hz) of the 3.45 nV/sqrt(Hz) total, and passes the 50 nV/sqrt(Hz) limit with a wide margin. The noise at DRN_A is 115.8 nV/sqrt(Hz), dominated by the 330 kOhm drain resistor R_R25 (73.7 nV/sqrt(Hz)). The entry gives no limit for DRN_A noise, so this value is reported without a verdict.

## Open points

1. The 300 Ohm limit is set by the output swing of the OPAx196 model. The swing curve is the TI model fit (CLAWP_OPAx196 in OPAx196.LIB), and it was not checked against the datasheet. A datasheet check of the output swing against load current would confirm the ceiling of 4.27 V.
2. The overshoot at 1 nF and 10 nF has a first-order explanation (output impedance 1.47 kOhm with the load capacitance, zeta 0.17 at 10 nF). An isolation resistor or a smaller load capacitance would need a simulation. The chtr values are a sweep, not a verified cable or heater capacitance.
3. The +5VP rail in these runs is 5.06 V to 5.10 V and changes with the heater load. This depends on the B5819W stand-in diode (see the quirks log, Batch 4), so the headroom figures are valid for that model only.
4. Closed from the data. The HFB_A error over vh_a = 0.5 V to 2.0 V is 0.028 mV to 0.032 mV (300 Ohm), 0.031 mV to 0.033 mV (600 Ohm) and 0.033 mV to 0.034 mV (1200 Ohm). This is 2 x 13.6 uV, the model input offset (0.027 mV), plus a residual of 0.6 uV to 6.4 uV that changes with the load and the rail. The residual is not explained. It is below 1 % of the 1 mV criterion and changes no verdict. The P33c offset from the formula is 18.4 uV to 18.6 uV. Any settling residue of the pseudo-transient bias point is far below the 1 mV criteria.
5. The P33c sweep uses a 0.05 V step. The index sweep (0.01 V) would take about 2.7 hours at the measured 34 s per point. The linearity criterion is checked on the 0.05 V grid. The deviation from the line changes by less than 0.2 uV between neighbouring points over the whole window, so a step in the deviation at the transition would show on this grid. The 0.05 V step is therefore adequate, and the index entry now states the run as done.
6. The index entry gives the linearity window as VSUP_A 0.5 V to 3.5 V, and the sweep stops at vdrc_a = 3.3 V. The analysis uses vdrc_a 0.46 V to 3.19 V, as given in the run request.
7. The compliance question for the drain (GMOS model TBD). A 0.5 V to 3.5 V VSUP_A window and a 7.5 uA drop across 330 kOhm (2.475 V) are inconsistent below VSUP_A of about 2.5 V plus the pixel compliance. The owner must decide whether the window or the pixel model is to be changed. This is a requirement question, not a simulation result.
8. The 100 nV/sqrt(Hz) DAC noise is an assumed source. It is not in the netlist and is added in the post-processing only.
9. The references AN-9, AN-10, AN-14 (LIA-Readout) and F-08, F-09 (2026-10-29 review) are not in `simulations/design-context.md`. They were taken from the entry and not checked against their source documents (not verified).
10. The P33d_noise_drn run was done as a second run of the same copy. The transfer used for DRN_A is from that run.
