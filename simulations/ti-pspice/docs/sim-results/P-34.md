# P-34 Interference and harmonic rejection with the Sallen-Key post-filter

## Method

**Interferer scaling.** The interferer is placed at BPF_OUT with amplitude ai = 0.4 V x |H(fi)| / |H(f0)|, so that an interferer and an f0 signal of equal amplitude at the drains give the same drain amplitude. The gains are from the ideal chain of `docs/sim-results/scripts/afe_eval_expected.py` (|H(518 Hz)| = 39.587 V/V). The script recomputes ai from the ideal chain, and the values agree with the circuit files to 0.001 %. The vendor models differ from the ideal chain by 0.00004 % at 50 Hz and by -0.024 % at 517.571 Hz (P-24). The vendor values at 500, 550, 1036 and 1554 Hz were not simulated, so the ideal values are used.

**Phase.** The interferer source starts with phase 0 at t = 0, and V_REFX starts high at t = 0. The 3f0 interferer has zero phase against the reference, so cos(phase) = 1.

**Products.** The square reference has odd harmonics n with Fourier weight 4/(n pi). Multiplying by sin(2 pi fi t) gives a difference product at |n f0 - fi| with weight +2 ai/(n pi) and a sum product at n f0 + fi with weight -2 ai/(n pi). The script uses n up to 39. Products at equal frequency add as real weights. The 1036 Hz interferer gives two products at 518 Hz (n = 1 and n = 3) with the same sign, so the amplitude is 8/(3 pi) ai, which is 2.5 dB above the single term of the first draft. The 3f0 interferer gives DC with weight 2 ai/(3 pi).

**Post-filter models.** The ideal post-filter is H_post(f) = 1 / (1 - (w tau)^2 + 2 j w tau), with tau = R C = 200 kOhm x 1.5 uF = 0.3 s. The feedthrough model adds the closed-loop output impedance of U26B. The current V(X_DEM) / R14 through C13 meets this impedance, so X_OUT = X_DEM(f) (H_post(f) + j w L / R14). The inductance L is taken from the reference run, as set out in the Discussion.

**Initial state.** The post-filter nodes start at their settled values (`.IC`, `.TRAN ... SKIPBP`). X_OUT starts at 0 V at t = 0 and reaches its level within 1 ms, because the output follows its input node. The start-up leaves a free response of the post-filter pair, which the fit removes.

**Fit.** Each run is resampled on a 5 us grid from 0.5 s to 1 s, and the reference is subtracted. The difference is fitted by least squares with a DC term, a cosine and a sine at every product frequency up to 4 kHz, and the free response of the pair, (d + e t) exp(-t/tau). The free response is needed. Without it the 3f0 DC shift is 3.1 dB away from the prediction. A second fit over 0.6 s to 1 s agrees with the first to 0.01 dB for the 18 Hz, 32 Hz, 518 Hz and DC terms. The 468 Hz term of P34_f50 changes by 1.2 dB (4.5 nV to 4.0 nV), which is inside the 1.9 nV floor of that run. The secondary lines of P34_f500 change by 0.4 dB to 1.1 dB, while those of P34_f550 change by at most 0.02 dB.

**Rejection.** The rejection in dB is 20 log10( amplitude at X_OUT / ((2/pi) x 0.4 V) ). The reference (2/pi) x 0.4 V = 0.254648 V is the X_OUT of an f0 signal with the same drain amplitude as the interferer.

**Floor.** The control-bin floor is the amplitude spectrum of the fit residual (2 Hz bins, 100 Hz to 4 kHz), with the product bins and the 2f0 harmonic bins removed (within 2 Hz). The floor is the mean plus three times the standard deviation of the remaining bins. The white-noise estimate of the first draft is not used.

The verdict uses two rules. The ideal rule compares the measurement with the ideal post-filter prediction of the first draft. The feedthrough rule compares it with the prediction including the Sallen-Key feedthrough. The entry's pass rule is within 2 dB against the prediction, or below the floor where the prediction is under -100 dB.

The verdict for the 50 Hz term is: FAIL against the ideal-post-filter prediction, PASS (within 0.6 dB) against the prediction with Sallen-Key feedthrough. SIM-15 limits pass.

The verdict for the 518 Hz term is: FAIL against the ideal-post-filter prediction, PASS (within 0.01 dB) against the prediction with Sallen-Key feedthrough. SIM-15 limits pass.

The verdict for the 2f0 ripple is: FAIL. The ripple is 452.9 nV against the limit of 0.1 uV. The feedthrough model gives the same value, so the failure is caused by the feedthrough, not by a numerical error.

The 18 Hz, 32 Hz and 3f0 DC terms agree with the ideal prediction to 0.01 dB.

The 1036 Hz prediction is -127.32 dB. The first draft and the entry gave -129.8 dB or -130 dB from the largest single product only. The coherent sum of n = 1 and n = 3 gives 8/(3 pi) ai |H_post(518 Hz)| = 109.6 nV, which is 2.5 dB above the single term. The index entry is corrected to -127.3 dB.

## Discussion

The Sallen-Key stage does not stop the high-frequency components as the ideal post-filter does. Above its corner, C13 is a short, so the current V(X_DEM) / R14 flows into the output of U26B. This output sees its closed-loop output impedance, which is inductive. In the reference run the 1036 Hz ripple has a quadrature part of 450.9 nV, and this gives L = 81.6 uH. The same value explains the 4f0 ripple (180.4 nV measured, 180.4 nV model) and every product of every interferer run.

The feedthrough gain is w L / R14 = 2.56e-9 f (f in Hz). This is the stopband floor of the stage. It is -111 dB at 1 kHz and rises 20 dB/decade. The 2f0 ripple of 452.9 nV is therefore a feedthrough of the demodulated ripple, not an artefact of the simulator.

The sidebands of the 500 Hz and 550 Hz difference runs, which the first draft attributed to numerical effects, are the same feedthrough acting on the higher harmonics. They form a flat comb of about 350 nV per line at 2 k f0 - 18 Hz up to the 100 kHz Nyquist frequency of the 5 us grid. The estimate 2 ai x 2 f0 L / R14 gives 336 nV per line. The rms of the comb scales with ai, at 10.4 uV for ai = 0.398 V and 98.9 nV for ai = 3.76 mV. This explains the residual of the first draft.

The 50 Hz result is 3.3 dB above the ideal prediction. The feedthrough model places it within 0.6 dB, which is the largest deviation of the feedthrough model in this entry. The control floor of this run is 1.9 nV (-162.6 dB), so the 468 Hz product (4.5 nV) is far above the floor and is a physical result.

The 518 Hz term of P34_f1036 agrees with the feedthrough model to 0.01 dB. The SIM-15 2f0 limit is met with a margin of 83 dB.

The rails of 4.6 V do not change these results. All signals stay within ±0.45 V, below the OPA2196 input transition region (1.60 V at 4.6 V, 2.00 V at 5.0 V). P-28 found the same gain error at 4.6 V and 5.0 V. Only the size of the feedthrough could change, through the output impedance of U26B. A rerun at 5.0 V is optional.

## Open points

1. The stopband of the Sallen-Key stage is set by the feedthrough, about 2.6e-9 f (-111 dB at 1 kHz, rising 20 dB/decade). The design report should decide whether this is acceptable for the 2f0 and higher-harmonic limits.
2. The edge comb of about 350 nV per line at 2 k f0 - 18 Hz is a consequence of the same feedthrough. The design report should check it against the noise budget.
3. The inductance L = 81.6 uH is derived from the TI OPAx196 model (`nom_pspti.lib`). The real closed-loop output impedance of the OPA2196 at the board gain has not been checked against the datasheet. The result depends on it.
4. The start-up `.IC` gives a free response of the post-filter pair, so the first 0.5 s of X_OUT and Y_OUT are not settled. Use a settled window, or the fitted offsets above, and not the first samples. The 5 uV gap of the X_OUT offset to P-28 is not explained.
5. The 2f0 ripple requirement (below 0.1 uV) and the 1036 Hz prediction correction belong to the design report, together with the stopband value of point 1.
