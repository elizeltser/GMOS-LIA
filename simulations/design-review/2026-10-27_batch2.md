## Summary

The simulations of batch 2 are consistent with theory. The hardware risks are therefore not in the models but in what the models cannot represent or were not asked. The main points are these.

1. The TMUX6136 is outside its rated supply (D-01). This is now confirmed against the full datasheet, not only the product page. The 5 V replacement TMUX1136 has 15 times the charge injection of the model, which the simulations have not tested.
2. The Capture front end (+/-5 V, grounds at 0 V) and the single-supply chain that was simulated (`chain_ss.cir`) are different circuits (new issue D-10). All of P-09, P-15, P-16 and P-17 describe a circuit that is not in the schematic.
3. All chain tests used a 1 mOhm sensor. A real sensor impedance changes the gain, adds noise through the 100 kOhm bias resistors and was never simulated (new issue D-11). A series resistance of 1 kOhm per leg already adds 10 % noise, and 10 kOhm adds 70 %.
4. The reference-ripple error of P-10 applies only when the demodulator reference is separate from the filter and in-amp reference. With one shared reference node, the whole chain tracks the ripple and the DC error is zero in the ideal case (derived in D-04, test R-02). The topology decides whether D-04 is a Major issue or a non-issue.
5. A square-wave sensor signal reads 1.28 % low against a sine calibration (P-17, D-12). The tolerance spread of this harmonic factor is only 0.037 % (1 sigma, 1 % R and 5 % C), so a known correction factor, or a square-wave calibration, removes the error.
6. The phase tolerance of the band-pass is the largest uncorrected error (D-08), but the R = sqrt(X^2 + Y^2) reading is insensitive to drift. A per-board calibration is needed for X and Y alone.
7. The noise estimate of P-15 is probably 7 % to 12 % optimistic against the INA828 datasheet (D-14), and a 16-bit ADC would add 110 times the front-end noise (D-13).
8. The OPA192 sits in its input-stage transition region at mid-supply (D-15). The unexplained -0.055 % gain and -0.13 deg phase deficit of P-09 is reproduced to within 0.03 deg by a few pF of input capacitance at the two filter followers. It is not an issue.

### D-01 TMUX6136 is not rated for a single 5 V supply (Critical, confirmed)

Evidence.

| Source | Value |

The simulation files then describe the schematic.

### D-02 INA828 input common-mode range on 5 V (Major, downgraded from Critical)

Evidence.

| Source | Value |
|---|---|
| INA828 SBOS792A, table 6.5, VCM | Operating input range (V-) + 2 V to (V+) - 2 V |
| Same, section 7.3.3 | "within 2 Volts of both power supplies", detailed curves in figures 48 to 51 and 55 to 58 (VREF = 2.5 V for the 5 V case) |
| Same, section 6.3 | Single supply 4.5 V to 36 V, so 5 V is within the rating |
| P-09 (P09b) | The model clamps at 2.00 V and 3.00 V. Within 1 mV from 2.02 V to 2.98 V |
| Same, table 6.5, output swing | (V-) + 0.15 V to (V+) - 0.15 V (VS = +/-15 V, RL = 10 kOhm). The 5 V value is not verified |
| Same, table 6.5, IB and IOS | 0.15 nA typical, 0.6 nA maximum at 25 C, 2 nA maximum over temperature |

The model range and the datasheet agree. The bias at 2.5 V leaves 0.5 V of margin to each edge, which is more than the 0.2 V Draft margin. The risk is therefore no longer a certain failure. It depends on the sensor common-mode voltage, which is TBD (`simulations/design-context.md` section 4), and on the dependence on the output voltage (figure 55), which could not be read from the PDF text (not verified).

When the common mode leaves the range, P-09 shows that the output falls back to the reference and the signal is lost. It does not clip. A lock-in would read zero without warning.

Output headroom with the Draft 0.2 V margin. The output may swing from 0.15 V + 0.2 V = 0.35 V to 4.65 V. Around 2.5 V this leaves 2.15 V. A 0.166 V amplitude (Draft) leaves 1.984 V for the DC part at the in-amp output, which is 1.984 V / 11 = 0.18 V of DC differential at the input (sensor DC, offset, bias current times source resistance). Otherwise use an in-amp with a wider range or level shift the sensor. Check the final case with TI's common-mode calculator. Status: open, Major until the sensor common mode is known.

### D-04 Reference ripple at f_osc (Major if the demodulator reference is separate, Minor if shared and verified)

New evidence from P-10 and a derivation not covered by it.

P-10 results (graded A): a 1 mV ripple at f_osc on Vref gives +0.6366 mV (X, phase 0) or -0.6363 mV (Y, phase 90 deg) of DC error, which is (2/pi) a. A reference derived from the rail (`kref` = 1) carries half the rail ripple (+0.3183 mV per 1 mV rail ripple). A 10 mA excitation step on a 1 Ohm rail with a rail-derived reference gives -2.500 mV. The rail values are test values (TBD in `simulations/design-context.md` section 1).

What P-10 does not show. In P-10 the input sine and the filter chain are ideal and do not move with Vref. If the in-amp REF pin, the filter returns and the demodulator reference are one node, the chain tracks the ripple. The demodulator output is Vref + s in the +1 state and 2 Vref - Vin = Vref - s in the -1 state, with Vin = Vref + s. The ripple is the same in both states and leaves no DC term, only a ripple at f_osc that the output RC attenuates by 1/sqrt(1 + (2 pi 517 Hz x 0.1 s)^2) = 0.0031. The nodal solution with ideal op amps (`calc6.py`) confirms that (VSAMPLE - Vref) / Vd stays at 11.0793 and +1.6300 deg for a reference resistance from 0.1 Ohm to 2.5 kOhm while the reference node moves by up to 4.44 times Vd.

| Topology | DC error for 1 mV ripple at f_osc |
|---|---|
| Demodulator reference separate from the filter and in-amp reference | 0.637 mV (P-10), 0.60 % of the 105.8 mV signal |
| One shared node (INA828 REF, filter returns, demodulator REF) | about 0, limited by tracking error. Estimate below 1.3 uV per mV. Test R-02 |

The tracking estimate assumes a tracking error of 2e-3 or less, which comes from the INA828 REF path and the phase lag of the followers (0.05 deg is 9e-4 rad). It is not simulated. Test R-02 measures it with an AC run.

Other coherent paths that remain. Differences between the reference potential at the in-amp, at the filters and at the demodulator caused by trace and ground-return impedance carrying the excitation current. In the separate-reference case, a coherent ripple of 0.31 uV amplitude (the 0.2001 uV rms DC-band noise of D-13 divided by 2/pi) already equals the SNR = 1 amplitude of 28.4 nV. It is a baseline error and not a noise error. It changes with the excitation current, which depends on the sensor. It therefore acts as a gain or nonlinearity error and cannot be removed by one baseline subtraction.

Reference noise. The REF3425 listing shows 24 uV rms noise from 10 Hz to 10 kHz (LCSC attribute, not verified against the datasheet). The average density is 24 uV / sqrt(9990 Hz) = 240 nV/sqrt(Hz), which is 1.9 times the DC-band density of the chain (126.56 nV/sqrt(Hz)) in a separate-reference topology. A filter before the buffer fixes it: 1.6 kOhm and 10 uF give a corner of 9.95 Hz and an attenuation of 0.0192 at 517 Hz, so 240 nV/sqrt(Hz) becomes 4.6 nV/sqrt(Hz). In the shared topology the reference noise is also tracked and cancels to the first order.

Recommended change. One reference node, low impedance, with a noise filter and buffer (see "Part recommendations"), distributed in a star to the in-amp REF pin, the filter returns and the demodulator reference. Do not derive the reference from the rail. Keep the excitation return current off this node. Test: R-02. Status: open.

### D-05 Resistor mismatch converts input offset into DC and scale error (Minor, Major with 1 % resistors)

New evidence. P-11 confirmed delta VX = -(delta/2) (ost + 2A/pi) within 0.0005 mV for delta = +/-0.1 % and +/-1 %, and VY unchanged.

With the filter chain in front, ost is below 0.25 mV (P-09: VDIFF offset 0.245 mV, blocked by the high-pass filter, later stages below 0.005 mV). So the offset term is (delta/2) x 0.25 mV = 0.125 uV at delta = 0.1 %, negligible. The scale term dominates: delta/2 of the signal.

| Resistor tolerance | Worst-case delta (R1 against R2) | Scale error of the channel |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1 % | 0.2 % | 0.1 % (at the Draft threshold) |
| 1 % | 2 % | 1 % (ten times the threshold) |

X and Y have separate resistor pairs, so their scale errors differ and the X/Y ratio errs by up to 2 x delta/2, which tilts the measured phase by up to atan(0.002) = 0.11 deg at 0.1 % and 1.1 deg at 1 %. The resistor tolerance is TBD in `simulations/design-context.md` section 6.

The simulated mismatch offset term (-0.153 mV per volt) contains the -0.103 mV/V duty artefact of the PSpice PULSE width (quirk "PULSE width is counted after the rise time"). It does not exist in hardware if the references come from a timer (F-C below).

Recommended change. Thin-film 0.1 % resistors, 25 ppm/K, in matched pairs (R1 and R2 of the same type and batch), see "Part recommendations". Per-board gain calibration also removes this term. Status: open.

### D-06 Demodulator input impedance switches at the reference rate (Minor)

New evidence. P-12: 10 Ohm in series gives -0.0998 % of X, 100 Ohm -0.985 %, 1 kOhm -8.71 %. 10 Ohm is at the Draft Major threshold.

The simulated chain drives the node from the OPA192 follower U3. Its closed-loop output resistance at 517 Hz is roughly the open-loop output resistance divided by the loop gain (10 MHz / 517 Hz = 19 300). The open-loop output resistance was not read (not verified), but even 1 kOhm gives 0.05 Ohm. So the node is safe. Keep the node directly on the op amp output and place the switch inputs and R2 and R4 close to it. Status: open, Minor.

### D-08 Filter phase tolerance at f_osc (Major if uncalibrated)

New evidence. P-14 (499 samples): phase std 3.443 deg and gain std 3.617 % for 1 % resistors and 5 % capacitors (test values, TBD in `simulations/design-context.md`). The numpy reference (my own run, 200 000 samples, `calc1.py`) gives phase std 3.36 deg, gain std 3.58 % and 77.3 % of samples beyond 1 deg. This rests on the Draft 1 deg limit.

Which tolerance is needed (200 000 samples, RG included at the resistor tolerance):

| Resistors | Capacitors | Phase std (deg) | Gain std (%) | Samples beyond 1 deg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 % | 5 % | 3.36 | 3.58 | 77.3 % |
| 0.1 % | 5 % | 3.30 | 3.48 | 76.9 % |
| 0.1 % | 2 % | 1.32 | 1.39 | 46.3 % |
| 0.1 % | 1 % | 0.66 | 0.70 | 13.4 % |
| 0.1 % | 0.5 % | 0.34 | 0.36 | 0.08 % |
| 1 % | 0 % | 0.66 | 0.87 | 13.5 % |
| 0.1 % | 0 % | 0.07 | 0.09 | 0 % |

The capacitors set the phase spread. Capacitors of 0.5 % would be needed to keep nearly every board inside 1 deg without calibration. No 33 nF C0G with a tolerance better than 5 % was found in the in-stock JLCPCB catalogue on 27 October 2026. So the practical answer is a per-board calibration of phase and gain.

Drift is a separate matter. A uniform shift x of all R and C values moves the phase by -4.55 deg per 1 % (-0.457 deg per 0.1 %) and the gain by -0.045 % per 1 %. Over a 50 K temperature range, C0G (30 ppm/K) and thin-film resistors (25 ppm/K) in the worst case add up to 55 ppm/K x 50 K = 0.275 %, which gives -1.26 deg of phase and only 0.012 % of gain. For comparison, X7R capacitors with the usual -15 % change over temperature (all four capacitors, resistors unchanged) would move the phase by +36 deg and the gain by -10 % (`calc1.py`). So:

- Reading R = sqrt(X^2 + Y^2) is insensitive to drift: the gain at 517 Hz is near the peak (520.7 Hz). Its remaining error comes from a static gain tolerance and is removed by calibration.
- Reading X alone needs a phase calibration, and its drift limit over a 50 K range with C0G is 1.3 deg, above the Draft limit.
- X7R or Y5V capacitors are not usable in this filter.

The -0.055 % gain and -0.13 deg phase deficit of P-09 (open point of P-09, P-14 and P-17). A shunt capacitance at the follower inputs reproduces it. Adding 6.4 pF (the OPA192 common-mode capacitance, SBOS620E table 6.7: ZIC = 1e13 Ohm || 6.4 pF) to each of the nodes N07629 and N08020 changes the ideal front end by -0.058 % in gain and -0.034 deg in phase (`calc4.py`). Adding the INA828's own -0.060 deg (datasheet bandwidth of 640 kHz at G = 10 gives -0.046 deg) gives about -0.09 deg to -0.10 deg, against -0.126 deg measured. The model connects its 6.4 pF capacitors to an internal common-mode node (OPAx192.LIB lines 120 to 122, `E_E1 MID 0 ...`), so whether the model actually presents this capacitance to ground is not verified. Test R-03 settles it with two AC points. Either way the size is 0.13 deg, one eighth of the Draft limit, and stray capacitance of a few pF on a real board gives a similar value. No action beyond the tolerance and calibration above.

Recommended change. C0G capacitors, thin-film 0.1 % resistors with 25 ppm/K, one-point phase and gain calibration per board with a reference tone through the same path (the calibration tone must have the same waveform as the signal, see D-12), and a temperature check of the calibration. Compute R for monitoring. Status: open.

### D-09 Simulations use a 2 V amplitude (Note, partly addressed)

New evidence. P-08 to P-13 run at 0.2 V and P-09 to P-17 at 15 mV in and 166 mV at VSAMPLE, as recommended. P-08 shows that the switching errors are consistent between 2 V and 0.2 V. The recommendation also asked for "the 5 V-rated parts". That part is not done, because the switch model still has the TMUX6136 values. Test R-04 closes it. Status: open until R-04.

### D-11 Sensor source impedance, bias resistors and noise were not simulated (Major, new, rests on a TBD sensor)

Evidence. `chain_ss.cir` uses `rsens` = 1 mOhm per leg (line 62) for every chain test (P-09, P-15, P-16, P-17). The sensor impedance is TBD (`simulations/design-context.md` section 1). Each INA828 input has a 100 kOhm resistor to VREF (lines 27 and 28). The INA828 itself needs a bias path (SBOS792A section 8.2), so the resistors are required. With a source resistance Rs per leg:

- The differential signal is divided by 200 kOhm / (200 kOhm + 2 Rs).
- Each leg adds thermal noise of the parallel resistance Rs || 100 kOhm, and the INA828 current noise (170 fA/sqrt(Hz), table 6.5) flows through it.

| Rs per leg | Gain loading | Noise added per leg (4kT(Rs\|\|100 k)) | Total, input-referred to the in-amp, with 12.68 nV/sqrt(Hz) base | Referred to the sensor EMF | A_min at tau = 0.1 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 mOhm | 1.0000 | 0 | 12.68 | 12.68 | 28.4 nV (P-15) |
| 100 Ohm | 0.9990 | 1.3 nV/sqrt(Hz) | 12.81 | 12.82 | 28.7 nV |
| 1 kOhm | 0.9901 | 4.05 nV/sqrt(Hz) | 13.92 | 14.06 | 31.5 nV |
| 10 kOhm | 0.9091 | 12.3 nV/sqrt(Hz) | 21.61 | 23.77 | 53.2 nV |
| 100 kOhm | 0.5000 | 28.8 nV/sqrt(Hz) | 44.30 | 88.6 | 198 nV |

The base value of 12.68 nV/sqrt(Hz) is the P-15 result. The noise values are computed in the scratchpad (`calc2.py`) with the two legs added in quadrature. The A_min column scales the 28.39 nV of P-15 with the EMF-referred density. The gain loading exceeds the Draft 0.1 % threshold from 100 Ohm per leg.

Consequence. The gain error is static and is calibrated out if the sensor impedance is constant. It is not constant for a gas sensor whose resistance is the measured quantity. A sensor impedance that changes with the gas changes the loading, and this is a signal-dependent gain error. The noise floor is also higher than P-15 states as soon as the source resistance reaches a few hundred Ohm.

Recommended change. Define the sensor impedance range (open question). Raise the bias resistors only as far as the INA828 current and the offset allow, or bias the inputs through the sensor itself if the sensor is DC coupled to a defined potential. Use larger bias resistors (1 MOhm) if the source is high impedance and accept the 0.6 nA maximum bias current times 1 MOhm = 0.6 mV offset, which the high-pass filter removes. Test R-01 measures the gain and noise with the sensor resistance. Status: open.

### D-12 Square-wave signals through the band-pass (Major, new)

Evidence. P-17: the square/sine VX ratio is 1.25700 (ideal 1.2570), and the X response to the square wave is 1.276 % below a calibration that treats the square as its fundamental. The Draft major threshold is 0.1 % (`simulations/design-context.md` section 7). The excitation is a square wave (Draft, section 1), but the sensor response waveform is TBD.

New numbers. The harmonic factor (square response divided by the fundamental-only response) is 0.98725 nominal. Under tolerances (4000 samples, `calc5.py`):

| Tolerance (R, C) | Factor mean | Std | Min to max |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 %, 5 % | 0.98720 | 0.037 % | 0.98583 to 0.98807 |
| 0.1 %, 1 % | 0.98725 | 0.007 % | 0.98704 to 0.98744 |
| 0.1 %, 0.2 % | 0.98725 | 0.002 % | 0.98721 to 0.98731 |

So the harmonic effect is well defined. The error is systematic and not random. A calibration with a sine tone leaves 1.275 % of error. A calibration with a square tone of the same shape and amplitude leaves the tolerance spread only (below 0.04 % with 1 % and 5 %).

The caveat is the assumption that the sensor delivers an ideal square. A sensor with its own time constant gives a different harmonic mix. The 1.27 % applies only to the ideal case.

Recommended change. Calibrate with the waveform that the sensor delivers (a square or the real response), or apply the factor of 0.98725 with a stated uncertainty. Do not use a sine calibration tone for a square-wave measurement. Status: open.

### D-13 ADC resolution and interface (Minor, new, rests on the TBD ADC)

Evidence. The noise in the DC band at VX (P-15) is 126.56 nV/sqrt(Hz) x sqrt(2.5 Hz) = 0.2001 uV rms for tau = 0.1 s. The DC output per volt of sensor amplitude is G x 2/pi = 11.07 x 0.6366 = 7.05 V/V, so the SNR = 1 amplitude of 28.4 nV is 0.2 uV at the output.

| ADC (full scale 5 V) | LSB | Quantisation noise (LSB/sqrt(12)) | Ratio to front-end noise |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 bit | 76.3 uV | 22.0 uV rms | 110 |
| 18 bit | 19.1 uV | 5.5 uV rms | 27.5 |
| 20 bit | 4.77 uV | 1.38 uV rms | 6.9 |
| 24 bit | 0.298 uV | 0.086 uV rms | 0.43 (ideal, no ENOB loss) |

Resolving 0.2 uV in 5 V needs log2(5 V / 0.2 uV) = 24.6 bits. Unless the resolution target is looser than 28 nV, the ADC needs a gain stage before it or a delta-sigma converter with a programmable-gain input. The resolution target is TBD, so the severity is Minor.

Two more points. The output resistor R3 is 10 kOhm. An ADC input current of 1 uA would drop 10 mV, which is 9.5 % of the 105.8 mV signal. A buffer or a high-impedance input is needed. The 2f ripple at VX is 1.306 mV peak at 0.2 V and tau = 10 ms (P-08), so about 1.306 x (0.166/0.2) / 10 = 0.108 mV at tau = 0.1 s and 166 mV. A first-order output filter attenuates 1034 Hz by only 1/(2 pi 1034 x 0.1) = 1.5e-3. The ADC sampling rate and filter must keep this ripple from aliasing into the DC band.

Recommended change. Choose the ADC after the resolution target. Add a buffer after R3 or use a delta-sigma ADC with a high input impedance. Status: open.

### D-14 The noise estimate is probably optimistic (Minor, new)

Evidence. INA828 SBOS792A table 6.5: input stage noise eNI = 7 nV/sqrt(Hz) at 1 kHz (G = 100, Rs = 0), output stage noise eNO = 90 nV/sqrt(Hz), noise current 170 fA/sqrt(Hz). Footnote 5: eN(RTI) = sqrt(eNI^2 + (eNO/G)^2).

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| INA828 at G = 11, RTI | sqrt(7^2 + (90/11)^2) = 10.77 nV/sqrt(Hz) |
| RG = 5 kOhm term | 4kT x 5 kOhm = 9.10 nV/sqrt(Hz), times 50 kOhm/55 kOhm = 0.909, gives 8.28 nV/sqrt(Hz) |
| In-amp with RG | sqrt(10.77^2 + 8.28^2) = 13.58 nV/sqrt(Hz) |
| Simulated chain total (P-15) | 12.68 nV/sqrt(Hz), including the filters and followers |

The factor 0.909 of the open point in P-15 is explained: the Johnson current of RG flows through the two 25 kOhm resistors, so the voltage noise referred to the input is 4kT RG x 2RF/(RG + 2RF), with 2RF = 50 kOhm. The factor is 50/55.

The simulated total is below the datasheet value of the in-amp alone. Adding 4 nV/sqrt(Hz) for filters and follower (an assumption, based on P-15's "rest") gives 14.2 nV/sqrt(Hz). The corrected estimate is therefore 13.6 to 14.2 nV/sqrt(Hz) (7 % to 12 % above the simulation), and A_min at tau = 0.1 s is 28.39 nV x (13.58 to 14.16)/12.68 = 30.4 nV to 31.7 nV. The model probably underestimates the output-stage noise eNO.

Options to lower the floor. The RG term and the output-stage term both fall with gain. For G = 26 (RG = 2 kOhm): sqrt(7^2 + (90/26)^2) = 7.8 nV/sqrt(Hz) and RG term 5.76 nV x 50/52 = 5.5 nV/sqrt(Hz), total 9.5 nV/sqrt(Hz), 30 % below the 13.58 of G = 11. The cost is headroom: at 15 mV in, the in-amp output amplitude is 0.39 V and the DC differential budget falls to (2.5 - 0.15 - 0.2 - 0.39) V / 26 = 68 mV. The sensor signal and DC level are TBD, so the gain is a decision for the user.

Demodulator noise. The P-15 estimate used 4.5 nV/sqrt(Hz) for the OPA192, which the judge corrected to 5.5 nV/sqrt(Hz). At the 5 V supply the demodulator op amps run at 2.5 V common mode, inside the input transition region where the datasheet gives 12.5 nV/sqrt(Hz) for the upper region (table 6.7, VCM between (V+) - 1.5 V and (V+) + 0.1 V). The value in the transition region is not given. Taking 12.5 nV/sqrt(Hz) as the worst case: sqrt((2 x 12.5)^2 + 12.87^2 + 12.87^2) = 30.9 nV/sqrt(Hz), 24 % of the DC-band density of the chain, 6 % of the noise power. A_min changes by 3 %. This is an upper bound.

Status: open, Minor.

### D-15 OPA192 at mid-supply on a 5 V rail (Minor, new)

Evidence. OPA192 SBOS620E, table 6.7 and section on common-mode range: the input stage uses two pairs. Both are on for (V+) - 3 V < VCM < (V+) - 1.5 V. On 5 V this is 2.0 V to 3.5 V. All three OPA192 stages of the chain (U2, U3 and the two demodulator op amps) work at 2.5 V, inside this region. The table gives no typical offset or CMRR for the region ("See Typical Characteristics"). It gives, at VCM = (V+) - 1.5 V, offset +/-25 uV typical and +/-150 uV maximum at 0 C to 85 C (+/-250 uV maximum over -40 C to +125 C). The 8 V to 36 V table is the one I read. The 4.5 V table has the same structure, but I did not confirm every value (not verified).

| Effect | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Static offset at VX, 2 Vos (P-13, entry gain 2) | 10 uV (Vos 5 uV typical) to 300 uV (150 uV maximum) | table 6.7 |
| Sensor-equivalent of 2 Vos | 10 uV / 7.05 = 1.4 uV to 43 uV, which is 50 to 1500 times A_min | `calc2.py` |
| Drift 0.2 uV/K x 2 over 10 K | 4 uV, 0.57 uV sensor-equivalent, 20 times A_min | JLCPCB listing, datasheet value of 0.1 uV/K to 0.5 uV/K typical is in table 6.7 |

The simulation shows a model offset below 2 uV (P-13), so it does not represent the transition region, or the model sample is a good one (not verified). The offset is static. It is a baseline term and not a gain error, and the lock-in (differential active/blind measurement) can remove it. The signal-dependent part is smaller. In the +1 state V+ moves by +/-0.17 V around 2.5 V and the offset changes with VCM inside the region. With CMRR of at least 86 dB (the lowest figure of the table, region not specified) the error is 0.17 V x 10^(-86/20) = 8.5 uV peak, which is 0.008 % of the signal.

Recommended change. Keep the OPA192 for the filters. For the two demodulator op amps, consider a zero-crossover zero-drift part (OPA388, see "Part recommendations"). The simulation check is cheap: the same netlists with the other model. Status: open.

### D-16 Fixed band-pass against the frequency requirement (Minor, new)

Evidence. `simulations/design-context.md` section 3 gives the frequency range as 100 Hz to 10 kHz (Draft). The front-end filters are tuned to 520 Hz (HP corner 380 Hz, LP corner 708 Hz, C-05). Gain of the ideal chain: 0.425 V/V at 100 Hz, 11.08 V/V at 517 Hz, 0.030 V/V at 10 kHz (`calc1.py`). The operating frequency f_osc = 517 Hz is not in `simulations/design-context.md`. It comes from the LIA netlist (P-08 open point 7).

Consequence. The design works at one frequency. Operation over 100 Hz to 10 kHz needs different filter values or switched filters. The two requirements contradict each other.

Recommended change. State f_osc in `simulations/design-context.md` or state the tunable range. Status: open (requirement question).

### Findings without an issue number

These are items that I checked and that need no register entry. They are listed because the checklist asks for them.

**F-A Switch charge injection and leakage (checked, no issue with a low-impedance reference).** The charge Q per edge is dumped into the node that D is connected to. The mean current is at most Q x 2 f_osc. For the TMUX1136 value of 6 pC: 6 pC x 2 x 517 Hz = 6.2 nA, against 0.41 nA for the TMUX6136 value of 0.4 pC. The DC error is that current times the resistance of the source it flows into.

| Source resistance at the switch | Error with 6 pC | Error with 0.4 pC |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Ohm (buffer plus trace) | 6.2 nV | 0.4 nV |
| 10 Ohm | 0.06 uV | 0.004 uV |
| 1 kOhm | 6.2 uV | 0.41 uV |
| 5 kOhm (divider) | 31 uV (0.03 % of 105.8 mV) | 2 uV |

With the buffered reference of D-03 and D-04 the charge injection is negligible. A divider reference would fail on this error as well. Leakage of 3 pA times 5 kOhm is 15 nV. Test R-04 checks the simulation numbers.

**F-B Stability and transients (checked, no issue).** The INA828 is stable to 1000 pF of load (table 6.5). The OPA192 drives 1 nF (feature list). The follower U3 drives two 10 kOhm resistors and two switch pins (about 40 pF). The demodulator op amps drive R1 and R3 with 10 kOhm. P-16 shows a recovery of 6.2 ms after clipping with no saturation memory, in line with the linear replay. The OPA192 overload recovery is listed as 200 (table 6.7, tOR, unit not confirmed in the text conversion, probably ns). Test R-05 extends this to a DC step at the input, which is the likely real overload event for a gas sensor.

**F-C Reference duty and switch timing (checked, no issue in hardware).** The -0.103 mV per volt of offset of P-11 comes from the PSpice PULSE width (T/2 + 100 ns). In hardware the references come from a timer (Draft). Take an edge error of 1 ns: 2 x 1 ns / 1.934 ms = 1.0e-6, or 1 nV per mV of offset. A switch tON and tOFF difference of 40 ns (the datasheet gives 66 ns typical and 78 ns maximum for the TMUX6136, and 12 ns to 19 ns for the TMUX1136) gives 2 x 40 ns / 1.934 ms = 4.1e-5, or 0.041 mV per volt of offset. With an offset of 0.25 mV at the demodulator input this is 10 nV. The Y reference at exactly T/4 from a timer also removes the 0.18 deg timing error of the 482.6 us delay.

**F-D Switch ratings that the model does not cover (checked, no issue after D-01).** TMUX1136 signal range is GND to VDD, and the signal is 2.5 V +/- 0.17 V. The logic input VIH of 1.49 V is met by 3.3 V logic. The absolute maximum supply of the TMUX1136 was not read (not verified), so the 5 V rail needs a tolerance check against the 5.5 V recommended maximum.

**F-E PCB leakage and layout (not simulated, minor).** The in-amp inputs sit on 100 kOhm resistors. A board leakage of 1 GOhm from a 5 V node to an input gives 5 nA, which would be 0.5 mV across 100 kOhm and is blocked by the high-pass filter. It shifts the in-amp operating point by up to 0.5 mV. Guard the two input traces and keep the 100 kOhm resistors at the connector. The INA828 RFI filter is at 53 MHz (table 6.5).

### R-02 Reference tracking with a shared reference node

- **Goal:** show that a ripple at f_osc on one shared reference node (INA828 REF, filter returns, demodulator reference) produces no DC error, and measure the tracking error that is left (D-04).
- **Schematic:** `circuits/chain_ss_R02.cir`, a copy of `chain_ss.cir` in which `V_VREF VREF 0 {vref}` is replaced by `V_VREF0 VREF0 0 {vref}` and `V_RIP VREF VREF0 SIN(0 {aref} {f_osc} 0 0 {pref}) AC 1`, and the parameters `aref=0 pref=0` are added to the `.PARAM` line. acmag = 0, asin = 15 mV (Draft level).
- **Parameters:**
  - Run R02a (linear): `.AC LIN 1 517 517`, probes `V([VSAMPLE])`, `V([VREF])`, `V([VDIFF])`, `V([HPF])`. `V_SIN` AC magnitude 0 (`acmag` = 0).
  - Run R02b (confirmation): fast, `.TRAN 0 0.15 0 5u`, `.STEP PARAM aref LIST 0 1m` with pref = 0, then a second run with pref = 90 deg. Probes `V([VX])`, `V([VY])`, `V([VREF])`, `.OPTIONS NUMDGT=8`, `.IC V(VX)=2.5 V(VY)=2.5`. Readout = VX and VY minus the mean of V(VREF) over the last 20 periods (as P-10).
- **Expected:**
  - Derivation. The in-amp, both filters and the demodulator are referenced to one node, so the demodulator input is Vref + s and the output is Vref +/- s. A ripple a on Vref leaves no DC term. The nodal solution with ideal op amps gives V(VSAMPLE) - V(VREF) = Vd x 11.0793 /1.6300 deg whatever the reference resistance (`calc6.py`). The finite loop gain of the followers (Aol at 517 Hz is 10 MHz / 517 Hz = 19 300) and the INA828 bandwidth (640 kHz at G = 10, a lag of 0.046 deg = 8.1e-4 rad) leave a tracking error of the order of 1e-3.
  - R02a: V(VSAMPLE) - V(VREF) for a 1 V AC ripple is below 2e-3 in magnitude. (For comparison, an isolated demodulator reference would give 1.) Pass: below 2e-3.
  - R02b: delta VX (pref = 0) and delta VY (pref = 90 deg) relative to mean Vref are below 0.005 mV for a 1 mV ripple. The separate-reference result of P-10 is 0.6366 mV, so the improvement factor is at least 127. Pass: both below 0.005 mV, and the ratio to 0.6366 mV is reported.
  - If R02a gives more than 2e-3, the analyst identifies the stage (V(VDIFF) - V(VREF), V(HPF) - V(VREF), V(VSAMPLE) - V(VREF)).
- **Status:** proposed.

### R-03 Origin of the -0.055 % and -0.13 deg front-end deficit

- **Goal:** separate the follower models from input capacitance as the cause of the P-09 deficit.
- **Schematic:** `circuits/frontend_mc_nominal.cir` (or `frontend_mc.cir` without `.MC`). Variants as edited copies:
  - R03a: `X_U2` and `X_U3` replaced by ideal buffers `E_U2 HPF 0 N07629 0 1` and `E_U3 VSAMPLE 0 N08020 0 1`. The INA828 stays.
  - R03b: as R03a plus a 6.4 pF capacitor from N07629 to VREF and a 6.4 pF capacitor from N08020 to VREF.
  - R03c: ideal buffers and an ideal in-amp `E_INA VDIFF VREF INP CM 11`. This is the wiring check.
- **Parameters:** `.AC LIN 1 517 517`, `.OPTIONS NUMDGT=8`, probe `VM([VSAMPLE])`, `VP([VSAMPLE])`. Three runs of a few seconds.
- **Expected:** R03c reproduces the analytic values 11.0793 V/V and +1.6300 deg within 0.001 %. R03a gives 11.0794 V/V and +1.570 deg (the INA828 gain of 11.000074 V/V and phase of -0.060 deg from P-09). R03b changes R03a by -0.058 % and -0.034 deg (`calc4.py`), so 11.0730 V/V and +1.536 deg. The measured P-14 nominal is 11.0732 V/V and +1.518 deg.
  - If R03a is already at 11.0732 V/V and +1.52 deg, the OPA192 model causes the deficit. If R03a is at 11.0794 V/V and +1.57 deg and R03b is within 0.02 deg of P-14, the follower input capacitance is the cause.
  Pass: R03c within 0.001 %. The result for R03a and R03b is reported, not judged.
- **Status:** proposed.

### R-04 Switch with 5 V part parameters and reference impedance

- **Goal:** rerun the demodulator with the TMUX1136 data of D-01 and measure the effect of charge injection into a reference with finite resistance (D-01, D-09, finding F-A).
- **Schematic:** `circuits/lia_v3_fast_R04.cir`, a copy of `lia_v3_fast.cir` with Vampl = 0.2 V, ost = 0, p = 0, kref = 0, and these edits. The instance lines of `X_U1` and `X_U3` use `RON=2 CS=10P CD=20P QINJ=6P TAU=5N ILK=3P`. `E_REF REF 0 VALUE ...` becomes `E_REF REFB 0 VALUE ...` with a new `R_RREFS REFB REF {rrefs}` and the parameter `rrefs=1m`. CS = 10 pF is an assumption, because the datasheet source-off capacitance was not read. A second copy `lia_v3_fast_R04b.cir` keeps the original switch parameters (QINJ = 0.4P) as the baseline.
- **Parameters:** fast, `.TRAN 0 0.15 0 5u SKIPBP`, `.STEP PARAM rrefs LIST 1m 10 1k 5k`, `.OPTIONS NUMDGT=8`, `.IC V(VX)=2.5 V(VY)=2.5`, probes `V(VX)`, `V(VY)`, `V(REF)`.
- **Expected:** the baseline step (rrefs = 1 mOhm) gives VX - 2.5 V = -127.325 mV and VY - 2.5 V = -0.344 mV (P-08), within 0.01 mV. The DC shift with rrefs is at most Q x 2 f_osc x rrefs (a bound, the sign and exact size depend on the edge order in the model):

  | rrefs | Bound, 6 pC | Bound, 0.4 pC |
  |---|---|---|
  | 10 Ohm | 0.06 uV | 0.004 uV |
  | 1 kOhm | 6.2 uV | 0.41 uV |
  | 5 kOhm | 31 uV | 2.1 uV |

  Pass: the VX and VY shifts relative to the rrefs = 1 mOhm step stay within the bound (+10 %) or 0.002 mV, whichever is larger. The analyst reports the measured value over the bound. The spike at node N00695 is not resolved with the 5 us step and is reported as not measurable. A finding of more than the bound means the charge model of SPDT_PREC differs from the assumption.
- **Status:** proposed.

### R-05 DC step at the sensor input

- **Goal:** measure the chain response and recovery to a sudden DC change at the sensor input (baseline shift, switch-on), including the input clipping of the in-amp.
- **Schematic:** `circuits/chain_ss_R05.cir`, a copy of `chain_ss.cir` in which `R_SP SP3 INP {rsens}` is replaced by `V_STEP SP3 SPS PULSE(0 {dvstep} {tstep} 1u 1u 1 2)` and `R_SP SPS INP {rsens}`, with `dvstep=0 tstep=20m` added. asin = 15 mV, cout = 1 uF (tau = 10 ms in the run).
- **Parameters:** `.TRAN 0 0.3 0 2u` (2 us as P-16, because 5 us fails when the in-amp clips), `.STEP PARAM dvstep LIST 0 50m 100m 300m`, `.OPTIONS NUMDGT=8`, `.IC V(VX)=2.5 V(VY)=2.5`, probes `V(VDIFF)`, `V(VSAMPLE)`, `V(VX)`, `V(VY)`.
- **Expected:** the step response of the ideal chain (INA828 gain 11, high-pass and low-pass of the netlist) has a peak of 3.14 V per volt of input step at 0.47 ms, and the output returns to within 1 mV after 5.1 ms (50 mV), 5.2 ms (100 mV). `calc3.py` gives these values.

  | dvstep | VDIFF step (11 x dvstep) | Peak abs(VSAMPLE - 2.5 V) | Time until within 1 mV |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 50 mV | 0.55 V | 0.157 V | 5.1 ms |
  | 100 mV | 1.1 V | 0.314 V | 5.2 ms |
  | 300 mV | 3.3 V, so VDIFF would reach 5.8 V | about 0.67 V (VDIFF clipped near 4.85 V, 2.35 V step x 0.285) | about 5.6 ms |

  The mean of VX and VY returns to the dvstep = 0 value within 0.01 mV from 240 ms after the step (24 tau). A DC input is rejected by the demodulator mean (0.5 v + 0.5 (2 Vref - v) = Vref). During the transient the VX ripple at f_osc is about 39 mV per volt of VSAMPLE excursion (tau = 10 ms), so 12 mV at the 0.314 V peak. Pass: the VSAMPLE peak within 15 % of the table (the 300 mV row within 30 %), recovery time within 1 ms of the table, and the final VX and VY within 0.01 mV. The analyst reports if the INA828 model clips the step at a value other than 4.85 V.
- **Status:** proposed.

## Simulation efficiency

| Topic | Suggestion | Saving |
|---|---|---|
| Monte Carlo of the filters (P-14) | Use numpy with the closed-form transfer function. My run of 200 000 samples reproduces the PSpice spread (phase std 3.36 deg against 3.44 deg, gain 3.58 % against 3.62 %) in seconds, against 1638 s for 499 PSpice samples. Use PSpice `.MC` only to validate the toleranced models once | 99 % of run time |
| Linear tests | Use `.AC` for any question about a linear chain (R-02a, R-03, R-01). A transient needs 0.15 s of simulation time. An AC point needs milliseconds | factor of 100 or more |
| Coherent ripple tests (P-10) | The result is (2/pi) a times a cosine of the phase for a separate reference. A single run per topology is enough, with the other values derived | 5 of 6 runs |
| Harmonic response (P-17) | The closed-form sum of the P-17 entry gave -132.94 mV against -132.872 mV from the 2 us transient. Use the closed form for tolerance studies (`calc5.py` took seconds for 4000 samples) | 2 us transient avoided |
| Overload recovery (P-16, R-05) | The analyst's linear replay matches the simulation. Run the transient once for the clipped case and the step response in numpy for the unclipped ones | half of the runs |
| Noise | `.NOISE` with `.STEP` of `rsens` (R-01) in one run | five runs avoided |
| Settling | Fast settings (tau = 10 ms) are right. Do not repeat tau = 0.1 s runs. The scaled values follow from the first-order formula | as now |
